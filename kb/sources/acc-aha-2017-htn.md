# 2017 ACC/AHA High Blood Pressure Guideline

> **Worked example, seeded from Claude's training knowledge — every
> statement below is `unverified` until checked against the published
> guideline. Do not rely on clinically before verification.**

## What it says

- BP categories: normal <120/<80 · elevated 120–129/<80 · stage 1
  130–139 or 80–89 · stage 2 ≥140 or ≥90 mmHg.
- Diagnosis should rest on an average of ≥2 readings on ≥2 occasions;
  out-of-office measurement (home BP or ABPM) is recommended to
  confirm diagnosis and titrate therapy.
- Drug therapy for stage 1: when clinical CVD or 10-yr ASCVD risk
  ≥10%; otherwise nonpharmacologic therapy first with reassessment.
  Stage 2: drug therapy (two first-line agents from different classes
  suggested) plus lifestyle.
- BP target: <130/80 mmHg for most treated adults.
- Nonpharmacologic interventions with stated approximate SBP
  reductions: weight loss (~1 mmHg per kg), DASH-style diet (~11 mmHg
  in hypertensives), sodium reduction (~5–6 mmHg), potassium
  supplementation (~4–5 mmHg), aerobic exercise (~5–8 mmHg),
  moderation of alcohol (~4 mmHg).

## Population & scope

Adults; US practice. Separate sections for specific populations
(older adults, DM, CKD, pregnancy) not yet captured here.

## Caveats & exceptions

- Lowered the diagnostic threshold from 140/90 (JNC 7) — reclassified
  a large share of US adults as hypertensive; treatment threshold is
  risk-based, not automatic.

## Context

Supersedes JNC 7 (2003). Known tension with ACP/AAFP guidance in
older adults, which has used higher treatment targets — capture that
source before writing the comparison. Informed by SPRINT
(src-sprint-2015).
