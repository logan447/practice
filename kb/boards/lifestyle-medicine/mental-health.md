# LM Boards — Emotional & Mental Health (Section 7)

## Objective 1 — Stress, stressors, ACEs, managing the stress response

- **Three stress types** [ref 1]: eustress (good), neustress
  (inconsequential), distress (threatening) — distress is acute
  (intense, brief) or chronic (lingering; body perpetually aroused).
- **Stressor**: perceived threat altering homeostasis; categories:
  psychological, physiological, social, bioecological [ref 1].
- **ACEs** [refs 2–3]: CDC — potentially traumatic events age 0–17;
  6 of 10 adults have ≥1. Examples: abuse/neglect, witnessing
  violence, family suicide attempt/death, household substance use/
  mental illness/instability (separation, incarceration). ACEs + adult
  life stress + SES → psychological distress (hierarchical-regression
  framework, Fig 7.1 [ref 2]).
- **Stress response** [refs 4–7]: fight-or-flight; sympathetic ANS;
  HPA-axis activation; acute hormones epinephrine/norepinephrine;
  chronic hormone **cortisol**. Symptoms: anxiety, palpitations,
  dyspnea, sweating, GI upset, sleep difficulty, tremors, etc.
  Chronic stress = sympathetic response outweighing parasympathetic
  relaxation response [refs 8–12].
- **Chronic stress harms** [refs 8, 13–20]: immune impairment,
  inflammation, ↓bone density, memory problems, ↑appetite/weight/
  abdominal fat, insulin resistance, ↑glucose/cholesterol/TG,
  clotting, poor wound healing, poor sleep, pain/fatigue, poor mood,
  unhealthy habit adoption, telomere shortening; etiologic in HTN,
  T2DM, heart disease.
- **Nonpharmacologic stress-management menu** [refs 21–35]: breathing
  (long exhale, abdominal), movement (therapeutic yoga, fitness, tai
  chi, qigong), meditation, nature, self-expression (music, singing,
  art, dance), community (volunteering, family/friends, spiritual
  practice), self-help reading, massage, music, blue-light/medical
  light-box therapy.

## Objective 2 — Emotional ↔ physical health pathophysiology

- WHO: mental disorder = clinically significant disturbance in
  cognition, emotional regulation, or behavior [refs 36–37].
- MDD/anxiety causes: non-modifiable (genetics, perinatal infection,
  environmental hazards, socio-political) [refs 38–43]; modifiable
  (stress, nutrition, financial strain, sedentary lifestyle,
  comorbidity, addictions, social connectedness) — risk rises
  dose-dependently with risk-factor count [refs 44–48]. LM targets
  the modifiable set; **apply only once the patient is medically
  stable and not at acute risk of harm** [refs 49–51].
- **MDD ↔ T2DM bidirectional** [ref 53]: insulin mediates tryptophan/
  tyrosine → serotonin synthesis [refs 54–56]; MDD alters HPA axis +
  subclinical inflammation [refs 7, 57]; diabetes-related suicidal-
  ideation risk factors: insulin use, duration ≥5 y, HbA1c ≥6.5
  [ref 61].
- **MDD ↔ CAD** [refs 62–66]: depression → platelet adherence,
  endothelial dysfunction, ↓HRV, worse adherence; MDD = independent
  risk factor, ↑cardiac risk 15–20% in CAD, doubles cardiac events;
  MDD OR 2.55 / GAD OR 2.47 for cardiac death/MI/arrest/
  revascularization over 2 years [ref 66].

## Objective 3 — Screening tools (high-yield)

- 80% of primary-care visits relate to stress and lifestyle [ref 67].
- **PSS (Perceived Stress Scale)** [refs 68, 72, 83]: Cohen 1983;
  10 items, 0–4 each (items 4, 5, 7, 8 reverse-scored); 0–13 low,
  14–26 moderate, 27–40 high stress. Occupational stress facts:
  downsizing ↑CVD deaths [ref 76]; long hours ↑heart disease
  [ref 77]; work stressors double T2DM incidence in women (only 20%
  attenuated by adjustment) [ref 78]; job strain ↑Framingham risk via
  smoking/inactivity/alcohol, not BP/lipids [refs 79–82].
- **Depression:** USPSTF 2016 — screen all adults (incl. pregnant/
  postpartum) and adolescents 12–18, **Grade B**, where support
  systems exist [refs 84–85]. **PHQ-2** (0–3 each; positive ≥3;
  sens 72%, spec 85%) → confirm with **PHQ-9**, HAM-D, or BDI-II
  [refs 86–88]. **DSM-5-TR MDD**: ≥5 symptoms nearly daily × 2 wk,
  change from baseline, ≥1 of depressed mood / anhedonia, plus
  weight/appetite change, insomnia/hypersomnia, psychomotor change,
  fatigue, worthlessness/guilt, concentration trouble, recurrent
  thoughts of death/SI [ref 89].
- **Anxiety:** USPSTF has **no recommendation** on adult anxiety
  screening (as taught). **GAD-2** (positive ≥3) → GAD-7, HAM-A, BAI,
  DSM-5-TR [refs 92–93]. Rule out anxiety before attributing symptoms
  to stress [ref 94]. DSM-5-TR GAD: excessive hard-to-control worry
  more days than not ≥6 months + ≥3 of restlessness, fatigue,
  concentration difficulty, irritability, muscle tension, sleep
  disturbance, with impairment [ref 89].
- **PHQ-4** combines both (0–2 normal, 3–5 mild, 6–8 moderate, 9–12
  severe; any score ≥3 → further assessment) [ref 95].

## Objective 4 — Self-management and resilience building

- Patient role [refs 96–97]: coping skills (stress plan, CBT skills,
  time management, positive psychology), creative hobbies, gratitude
  journal, spiritual needs [refs 98–99], screen-time limits [refs
  100–101], social support (face-to-face especially for adolescents
  [ref 102]), written support-needs plan, community/online resources,
  mental-health referral as needed.
- Practitioner role [ref 103]: stabilize medically first → then
  self-management, cognitive restructuring, relapse prevention;
  celebrate progress; **action plan** = SMART goal + social support
  ("walk 3×/wk 20 min to cope with [challenge]; follow-up in 2 wk;
  husband walks with me"); **relapse-prevention plan** for
  predictable hard times.

## Objective 5 — Mindfulness, MBSR, CBT evidence

- **Mindfulness** [ref 104]: paying attention on purpose to thinking
  and feeling; presence in the moment; awareness of surroundings;
  withholding judgment. Awareness of stimulus-response space.
- **MBSR — Jon Kabat-Zinn** [ref 105]: >800 programs; ~9 classes/8
  weeks; ~45 min in-class practice; expectation of 45 min home
  practice 6 d/wk after; formal (meditation) + informal
  (attentiveness to routine activities).
- **2014 meta-analysis (47 trials, n=3,515)** [ref 106]: meditation
  programs — **moderate evidence** for improved anxiety, depression,
  pain; low for stress/distress and QoL; low/none for mood,
  attention, substance use, eating, sleep, weight; **no evidence
  meditation beats any active treatment** (drugs, exercise, other
  behavioral therapy); no harm.
- Neuroscience: ~700 new neurons/day adult neurogenesis [ref 107];
  8 weeks of training → ↑prefrontal cortex thickness, ↑hippocampal
  gray matter, ↓amygdala density (stress/anxiety) [refs 112–113];
  stronger prefrontal-amygdala connections [ref 114]; two
  self-reference modes (extended vs momentary) separable with
  training [ref 111].
- Clinical benefits [refs 74, 115–121]: hardiness, chronic pain,
  anxiety, depression, addiction, relationships, compassion/empathy,
  loneliness in older adults.
- **CBT** [refs 122–140]: arguably gold-standard evidence-based
  psychotherapy; structured, present-focused, reframes automatic
  negative thoughts. Neural effects: healthy connectivity, empathy,
  prefrontal gray matter, normalized reactivity (anxiety). Treats
  depression, anxiety spectrum (PTSD, OCD, panic, GAD, social/specific
  phobia), personality disorders, marital issues, substance use.
  Vs antidepressants: **as efficacious; less relapse after
  discontinuation; combination CBT+medication best** for MDD, panic,
  OCD [refs 138–140].

## Objective 6 — LM treatment plans for depression and anxiety

- LM mental-health plans fit chronic management/prevention; medical
  stability first; apply alongside medication/psychotherapy
  [ref 141]. Whole-person 6-pillar approach.
- **Firth 2019 meta-review** (Fig 7.2) [ref 142]: physical activity —
  protective vs bipolar; treats ADHD, anxiety, MDD, psychotic
  disorders; not smoking — protective vs ADHD, MDD, bipolar,
  psychosis; dietary pattern — protective vs MDD and treats it;
  sleep — protective vs bipolar, anxiety, ADHD; treats depression.
- **Depression plan** (APA 2019 guideline, adults 18–64) [refs
  143–148]: initial — psychotherapy or 2nd-gen antidepressant or
  combination (CBT/interpersonal + SGA); pharmacotherapy = standard
  of care for moderate-severe MDD; SI/HI → emergent stabilization
  first. APA-acknowledged alternates/adjuncts in specific adult
  populations: **exercise monotherapy, St. John's wort monotherapy,
  bright light therapy** [refs 149–150], yoga, acupuncture +
  pharmacotherapy; insufficient evidence: tai chi, omega-3
  monotherapy, SAMe monotherapy. LM additions: address causes —
  exercise (comparable to CBT [ref 153]; promising in elderly [refs
  154–155]; preventive in youth [ref 156]; better than no treatment
  but not superior to psych/pharm [ref 157]), sleep [refs 158–159],
  risky substances, stress management, CBT, meditation [refs
  161–163], psychotherapy.
- **GAD/panic plan** [ref 164]: first line — psychotherapy (CBT best
  evidence), pharmacotherapy (SSRIs; benzodiazepines short-term with
  great caution — addiction/tolerance [ref 161 as taught]), physical
  activity [refs 165–166]; plus sleep [ref 167], substance reduction,
  stress management, provider relationship [ref 168], meditation.
- **Nutrition & mental health** [refs 169–200]: *Lancet Psychiatry*
  2015 — "diet is as important to psychiatry as it is to cardiology"
  [ref 169]. **Patterns beat nutrients** [ref 170]: produce/whole
  grains/fish/olive oil/low-fat dairy/antioxidant pattern ↓depression;
  red-processed meat/refined/sweets/high-fat gravy pattern ↑; fast
  food HR 1.36 [ref 171]; trans fat ↑, MUFA/PUFA weakly ↓ [ref 175].
  RCTs: plant-based (no meat/fish/eggs) improved depressive symptoms
  in 2 weeks [ref 176]; "improved diet" (SMILES-style, whole-food)
  relieved major depressive episodes [ref 177]; fruit/veg →
  eudaimonic well-being, optimism, mood, workplace productivity
  [refs 178–189]. Specific nutrients (supplements unregulated,
  toxicity possible [ref 190]): omega-3 (bipolar, PTSD, depression,
  psychosis prevention; maternal depression link) [refs 191–193];
  SAMe adjunct to SSRI (N=73 RCT) [ref 194]; St. John's wort — mild-
  moderate MDD, ≈ antidepressants in 2016 review [ref 195]; folate
  speeds/improves antidepressant response [ref 196]; saffron,
  turmeric, probiotics, flaxseed, walnuts, carbohydrate-rich evening
  meals [refs 56, 197–199]; deficiencies (folate, B12, Ca, Fe, Se,
  Zn, omega-3) more common in depressed women [refs 192, 200].

## Objective 7 — Referral to mental health professionals

Refer when [refs 201–202]: active SI/HI (medical emergency —
immediate attention ± hospitalization); prior suicide attempt, family
history of suicide, or risk signals (feeling a burden, isolation,
talking of wanting to die, seeking lethal means); clinical judgment;
care beyond scope (e.g., certain prescriptions); no improvement/
relapse despite adequate treatment; comorbid mood disorders (bipolar,
OCD, personality disorder); unclear diagnosis; patient preference
with accessible care; behavioral therapy indicated in the plan
(MDD, anxiety disorders).

## Objective 8 — Mindfulness in the clinical encounter

- Skills: **presence** (sustained attention) [refs 103, 203–208] —
  attention worsened by mind-wandering, negative mood, ADHD/MDD/
  anxiety/PTSD/OSA, poor lifestyle, medications, multitasking;
  **clarity**; **curiosity** → open-ended questions, feedback
  receptivity, fewer decision errors, less confirmation bias, more
  innovation [refs 209–211].
- Clinician mindfulness benefits [refs 118, 212–226]: ↓rumination,
  memory, focus, ↓reactivity, cognitive flexibility, relationship
  satisfaction; for HCWs: empathy, compassion, counseling skills,
  QoL, ↓psychological distress.
- Point-of-encounter self-care [refs 227–229]: manage emotional
  energy, clear the mind before the room, self-compassion ("doing the
  best I can with the resources I have"), elicit positive emotions,
  stay present, work collaboratively, recover after intense focus.
- Carson, *Your Creative Brain* — 7 steps [ref 230]: open mind,
  imagination, divergent thinking, reason, recognize the useful,
  use emotion creatively, peak performance (goal + feedback +
  challenge matched to skill).
- Patient-facing micro-practices [ref 118]: body scan without
  changing anything; allowing/accepting sensations; couples
  eye-contact exercises (notice reactions; send kindness).
