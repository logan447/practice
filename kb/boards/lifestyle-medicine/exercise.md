# LM Boards — Physical Activity (Section 6)

## Objective 1 — Components of physical activity

- Four exercise types: **aerobic/endurance, resistance/strength,
  flexibility, balance** [ref 1].
- Aerobic benefits: ↓all-cause mortality, ↓CVD incidence/mortality,
  ↓multiple cancers, better QoL and sleep.
- Resistance benefits [ref 2]: ↓injury, ↑basal metabolic rate, ↓fatigue,
  better sleep, bone density (early post-menopause), body-fat and
  weight maintenance, lipids/glucose control, ↓arthritis pain,
  ↓hepatic steatosis in NAFLD without visceral-fat change [ref 3],
  ↓falls, mental health [ref 4], ↓low-back pain [ref 5].
- Table 6.1 contrasts (aerobic vs resistance): lean mass — no change
  vs moderate ↑; strength — minimal vs large ↑; VO2max — large vs
  minimal ↑; resting HR — moderate ↓ vs no change; both: insulin
  sensitivity moderate ↑, HDL small ↑, BMD moderate ↑.
- Definitions: hypertrophy (synthesis > breakdown), endurance, power
  (force × velocity), strength (1-RM), muscle quality (force per unit
  tissue) [refs 6–9].
- Flexibility: joint-specific; evidence for recovery/injury prevention
  is non-significant — hard to quantify intensity [refs 10–12].
- Balance training → falls: **37% ↓ fall injuries, 43% ↓ severe
  injury falls, 61% ↓ fracture falls** [ref 13].

## Objective 2 — Activity, inactivity, and health outcomes

- Physical inactivity = **4th leading risk factor for global
  mortality** [refs 1, 14]; poor cardiorespiratory fitness was the #1
  attributable fraction of all-cause mortality in the Aerobics Center
  Longitudinal Study (16–17%, more than smoking/HTN/DM/obesity)
  [ref 15].
- Inactivity causes >7% of all-cause/CVD deaths worldwide; US 8.3%;
  disease burden: 6% CHD, 7% T2DM, 10% breast and colon cancer
  [refs 16–18]. Only ~1 in 3 US adults meets guidelines [ref 23].
- **Terminology box (exam favorite):** physically active = ≥150
  min/wk MVPA; insufficiently active = >0 and <150; inactive = 0;
  sedentary behavior = waking activity ≤1.5 METs seated/reclined/lying
  [ref 21].
- Sitting: 6.9% of all-cause mortality attributable [ref 25]; harms
  independent of exercise; >8 h/d sedentary vs <4 h/d HR 1.52 without
  MVPA [ref 34]; TV ~HR 1.5 even with 7 h/wk exercise [ref 27].
  Counsel BOTH: be active AND break sitting every 50–60 min.
- Dose-response [ref 19]: most benefit within 10–20 MET-h/wk; plateau
  ~80 MET-h/wk; Table 6.3 HRs: 0.1–3.74 MET-h 0.81 → 22.5+ 0.59.
  Years of life gained (age ≥40): 75 min/wk brisk walking ~1.8 y;
  150 → ~2.5 y; 300 → ~3.2 y (figures as taught); standing 2 h/d
  ~10% mortality ↓ [ref 32]; NHS: 1–1.9 MET-h/wk already RR 0.82
  [ref 33]; **weekend warrior**: 150 min in 1–2 days HR 0.70 vs
  sedentary (spread >2 days HR 0.65; injury risk higher if crammed)
  [refs 34–35].
- Pathophysiology: arterial compliance, SR calcium handling, ↓CRP,
  antioxidant defense [refs 43–46]. **Exerkines** [ref 45]: signaling
  molecules from muscle/cardiometabolic/nervous/immune tissue +
  microbiome/bone; GLUT4 translocation (acute) and ↑GLUT4 content
  (training) for glucose control [ref 47]; **BDNF** ↑ with acute
  exercise → learning/memory, less depression/anxiety [ref 48].
- Economics: each added activity day ↓ median healthcare cost 4.7%;
  inactive person costs +$1,543/yr [ref 51]; $500–5,000 moves one
  person inactive→active; brief advice is most cost-effective
  [ref 52].

## Objective 3 — Benefits in special populations

- **Older adults:** follow adult guidelines as able; resistance ≥2×/wk
  (benefits larger than in younger adults; even centenarians gain
  mass); balance ≥3 d/wk; supervised group programs (tai chi) cut
  fall risk [ref 53]. Sarcopenia: muscle mass falls ~3.7–4.7%/decade
  from 20–30, 6.5–7%/decade after 75; strength falls 2–5× faster than
  mass and predicts disability/death better; muscle **quality** >
  mass [refs 54–59].
- **Pregnancy:** 150 min/wk moderate aerobic, spread out; benefits:
  less excess gain, gestational DM, gestational hypertensive
  disorders, depression, surgical delivery, preterm/low birth weight
  [ref 60]. Avoid: heat stress, supine after 1st trimester, spinal
  loading/hip hinging lifts if back pain [ref 61], falling/contact
  risks (collision sports, horseback, downhill ski, soccer,
  basketball). **Absolute contraindications** [ref 62]: severe
  respiratory disease, severe cardiac disease with exercise
  intolerance, uncontrolled/severe arrhythmia, placental abruption,
  vasa previa, uncontrolled T1DM, IUGR, active preterm labor, severe
  preeclampsia, cervical insufficiency. Relative: mild cardiac/
  respiratory disease, well-controlled T1DM, mild preeclampsia, PPROM,
  placenta previa after 28 wk, untreated thyroid disease, severe
  eating disorder, malnutrition, heavy smoking w/ comorbidity.
  Obesity in pregnancy: supervised PA+diet → ~0.9 kg less gain
  [ref 63].
- **IHD prevention:** active men/women ↓ primary CHD 20–30% [ref 64];
  active commuting HR 0.892 [ref 66]; activity type matters less than
  doing it.
- **T2DM prevention:** 150 min/wk moderate suffices; cohorts: −31%
  (unadjusted BMI) / −17% (adjusted) [ref 67]; structured exercise:
  HbA1c falls, more if >150 min/wk; advice alone works only with
  dietary co-intervention [ref 68].
- **Cancer:** colon −17–30% [ref 70], breast ~−25% most-vs-least
  active; during/after treatment improves fitness, symptoms, QoL,
  survival [refs 1, 69, 71].
- **Disability:** be as active as able; SPPB (gait speed, balance,
  chair rise) predicts mobility; structured programs → 18% ↓ physical
  disability [ref 72]. Parkinson's: tai chi beat stretching and
  resistance for stability [ref 73].
- **Obesity (ACSM)** [ref 74]: weight maintenance >250 min/wk;
  60–90 min/d moderate; strength 2×/wk (aerobic matters more for
  weight). PA alone → modest loss (150–225 min/wk ≈ 2–3 kg;
  225–420 ≈ 5–7.5 kg) [refs 77–81]; compensation halves expected
  loss [ref 78]; 1 mile ≈ 100 kcal; 1 lb fat ≈ 3,500 kcal; **NEAT**
  can add ~350 kcal/d [ref 83]; activity is the strongest predictor
  of long-term weight maintenance [ref 82]; **NWCR:** successful
  maintainers (avg 66 lb × 5.5 y) exercise ~1 h/d [ref 87]; fitness
  protects independent of adiposity — active obese ≈ inactive
  normal-weight for years of life lost [ref 19].

## Objective 4 — Physical Activity Guidelines (PAGA 2018, 2nd ed.)

- 2008 original; 2018 update [refs 1, 39]. Key 2018 changes: **10-min
  bout minimum removed** (all movement counts); immediate benefits
  (sleep, anxiety, BP, insulin sensitivity, executive function from
  single bouts); risk reduction starts within days–weeks.
- Doses: children 3–5 — active all day; 6–17 — **60 min/d MVPA** with
  vigorous ≥3 d/wk + muscle/bone-strengthening ≥3 d/wk; adults —
  150–300 min/wk moderate or 75–150 vigorous + strength ≥2 d/wk;
  older adults — same as able, add balance, light weights 10–15 reps;
  pregnancy — 150 min/wk moderate; chronic conditions — 150–300 as
  able, avoid inactivity.
- Table 6.4 additions (2018, bold outcomes): cancer sites expanded to
  bladder, endometrium, esophagus, kidney, stomach, lung; dementia
  risk ↓; fall injuries ↓ (older adults); pregnancy benefits;
  condition-specific (breast/colorectal/prostate cancer mortality ↓,
  osteoarthritis pain ↓, HTN progression ↓, T2DM indicators ↓, MS
  walking, cognition in ADHD/schizophrenia/MS/Parkinson's/stroke).
- Flexibility: no proven health-risk reduction; doesn't count toward
  aerobic/strength minutes; stretch program: 10 min, 2–3 d/wk (daily
  best), 10–30 s holds (older adults 30–60 s), 2–4 reps to 60 s per
  group [refs 1, 89]. Types: dynamic (pre-exercise), static
  (post-exercise), ballistic (generally not recommended — injury),
  PNF (contract-relax with partner) [refs 10, 90].

## Objective 5 — Assessment in clinic

- **PAVS (Physical Activity Vital Sign): days/wk × min/day = min/wk**
  of aerobic MVPA; Kaiser documented it at 86% of 1.79M visits
  [ref 91]. Interpretation: meets standard → congratulate, consider
  balance/flexibility; insufficient → acknowledge, assess readiness,
  close follow-up; sedentary → barriers discussion, relate to health
  problems, refer to community resources.
- **Intensity**: talk test (moderate: talk not sing; vigorous: few
  words) [ref 92]; respiratory rate; **Borg RPE 6–20** (moderate
  12–13, vigorous 14–17) / OMNI 0–10 (moderate 5–6) [refs 93, 96];
  **HRR 40–85%** (HRR = MHR − RHR; MHR = 220 − age); %maxHR: light
  ≤63%, moderate 64–76%, vigorous ≥77% [ref 97]; METs: light <3,
  moderate 3–5.9, vigorous ≥6 [ref 74]. One-rep-max %: moderate
  50–69%, vigorous 70–84%; RIR moderate 4–6, vigorous 2–3 [refs
  100–101]. Quick tip: a patient who walked in and is asymptomatic
  can do low-level activity.
- **Functional capacity:** 6-minute walk test (30 m hallway; compare
  patient to self) [ref 102]; 8-foot up-and-go (older adults).
  Cardiorespiratory: step test (12-inch, 3 min, recovery HR vs norms)
  [refs 103–104]; **Rockport 1-mile walk** (VO2max estimate within
  ±0.33 L/min) [ref 105]; Cooper 12-min run (VO2max = 35.97 × miles −
  11.29); FRIEND registry for norms [ref 106].
- Muscular: 1-RM (reliable), grip strength (predicts mortality/
  function in older adults [ref 107]), endurance (max squats/push-ups/
  sit-ups vs norms). Flexibility: sit-and-reach (hamstring r = 0.64;
  poor for low back r = 0.28) [ref 109]. Body composition: skinfolds
  ±3.5% (norms ~10–22% men, 20–32% women); densitometry (DEXA,
  underwater) = gold standard; BIA variable [ref 74].
- **Screening:** death during activity ~1 per 1.5M (men) / 36.5M
  (women) episode-hours of vigorous exercise [refs 110–111]; acute MI
  risk during/after vigorous exercise ~50× in habitually sedentary
  vs regular exercisers [ref 112] — but the universal risk is
  sedentariness ("are my patients safe to remain sedentary?").
  ACSM 2015 preparticipation logic [ref 113]: clearance depends on
  (1) current activity, (2) CV/metabolic/renal disease or signs,
  (3) desired intensity. **PAR-Q+** self-screening reduces barriers
  and false positives [refs 114–115; eparmedx.com]. Algorithm:
  regular exerciser, asymptomatic, no disease → no clearance; known
  disease, asymptomatic, exercising → moderate OK, clearance for
  progression to vigorous if not cleared in past 12 mo; symptomatic →
  stop, clear first. Non-exerciser: no disease → start light-moderate;
  known disease or symptoms → clearance first.
- **Stress testing:** ACC — increased-risk individuals before
  moderate-vigorous programs (conflicting evidence); USPSTF —
  **against routine testing of asymptomatic adults** (insufficient
  evidence). Best for intermediate-risk symptoms [ref 118]; not for
  low/high risk or asymptomatic ischemia screening; 1/10,000 tests →
  sudden cardiac death or hospitalization; contraindicated: MI <2
  days, hypertensive emergency, acute infection near heart,
  decompensated HF, unstable angina, severe symptomatic AS,
  uncontrolled arrhythmia, dissection [ref 117].

## Objective 6 — Fitness terminology and prescription mechanics

- Definitions [refs 21, 116, 119–122]: physical activity vs exercise
  (purposeful, planned, recurring); fitness (health-related:
  body composition, cardiorespiratory endurance, strength, endurance,
  flexibility; skill-related: agility, coordination, balance, power,
  reaction time, speed); deconditioning; **progressive overload**;
  **specificity**; **reversibility**; individuality; **NEAT**.
- **METs:** 1.0 = rest (~3.5 mL O2/kg/min); sleeping 0.9; 3-mph walk
  3; two flights of stairs 5; running 10.9 mph 18. MET-h/wk =
  METs × h/wk (brisk walk 3.3 METs × 4 h = 13.2). METs are absolute —
  they misclassify intensity as fitness improves.
- **FITT / FITT-VP:** Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type (+ Volume,
  Progression) [ref 74]. Example script: walking at talk-not-sing
  intensity, 30 min, 5 d/wk = 150 min/wk; progress time → hills →
  sixth session.
- ACSM progression for sedentary low-risk [ref 74]: initial stage
  1–6 wk (40–60% HRR, 15→30 min, 3–4×/wk, RPE 4–6/10);
  improvement 4–8 mo (raise max exercise HR ≤5% of HRR every 6th
  session as tolerated; duration +≤20%/wk until 20–30 min continuous);
  maintenance ≥24 wk (3–5×/wk, RPE 8+, 20–60 min; ≥50th percentile
  fitness goal).
- **Resistance prescription (ACSM FITT)** [ref 8]: 2–3 nonconsecutive
  d/wk; novice 40–50% 1-RM, intermediate 60–80%, experienced 80%;
  8–12 reps (older/frail 10–15 at lighter weight; endurance 10–15);
  2–4 sets (novices benefit from 1); rest 2–3 min between sets, 48 h
  between muscle groups; compound multi-joint > single-joint; core;
  agonist+antagonist pairs; progress resistance/reps/frequency
  gradually. Start RPE 5–6/10 with 4–5 RIR, 8–12 reps pre-fatigue.
  Only 23.5% of Americans meet both aerobic+strength guidelines.
- Flexibility/balance intro: stretching gives short-term ROM gains
  [ref 124]; balance — start with hand-hold support, remove support,
  unstable surface; assess with **4-Stage Balance Test** (side-by-side,
  offset, tandem, single-leg; 10 s each) [ref 125].

## Objective 7 — Exercise in treatment protocols (evidence)

- **DPP** [ref 126]: n=3,234 prediabetes; lifestyle (7% weight loss,
  150 min/wk) ↓ incidence **58%** vs metformin 31%; NNT 3-yr: 6.9
  lifestyle vs 13.9 metformin.
- **Look AHEAD** [ref 127]: largest T2DM lifestyle RCT; ILI (≥7%
  loss, ≥175 min/wk) → sustained weight, fitness, glycemic/BP/lipid
  gains with fewer medications.
- Resistance exercise alone can drop HbA1c ~0.5%+ (≈ many drugs)
  [ref 98].
- **Exercise vs drugs meta-epidemiological study** (305 RCTs, 339k)
  [ref 128]: secondary CHD prevention and prediabetes — no
  statistical difference vs statins/BB/ACEi/antiplatelets; stroke —
  exercise superior to anticoagulants/antiplatelets (OR ~0.09–0.10);
  heart failure — exercise **inferior** to diuretics (OR 4.11).
  Caveats: few head-to-head trials; AEs under-reported; statin+
  exercise may blunt/myalgia [ref 129].
- Exercise vs **PCI** (stable CAD, n=101) [ref 130]: 88% vs 70%
  event-free survival at 12 mo, +16% VO2max, cheaper, higher HDL.
- 8-week combined programs: BP −4 mmHg, strength +4/+11 kg, lean mass
  +0.8 kg; aerobic-only: VO2max +7.7, weight −1 kg; resistance-only:
  waist −1.7 cm [ref 131]. **≥1 h/wk resistance training → 40–70% ↓
  CVD events independent of aerobic activity** [ref 132]. BP
  meta-analysis of 391 RCTs: exercise −4.84 vs antihypertensives
  −8.80 mmHg SBP [ref 133].
- Cancer: exercise beneficial before/during/after treatment, moderate-
  vigorous most efficacious, AEs uncommon [ref 134]; 18-wk supervised
  program during breast/colon treatment → better activity/fatigue at
  4 y [ref 135]; 16-wk combined training in survivors → QoL, VO2max,
  strength, bone markers [ref 136]; 1.44M adults: ↓risk of 26 cancer
  types [ref 137].
- Stroke: aerobic (±resistance) → walking speed +6.71 m/min, capacity
  +30.29 m/6-min, neuroplasticity potential [refs 138–139]; combined
  resistance+balance → Berg +2.5 at 2×/wk × 3 mo [ref 140]. Despite
  all this, exercise interventions remain underused in medicine
  [ref 141]; ACSM resources guide safe prescription [ref 142].

## Objective 8 — Referral to fitness professionals

- Team: physical therapists (graded therapeutic exercise; needs
  diagnosis, insurance-limited visits), occupational therapists
  (ADLs, upper extremity), **clinical exercise physiologists**
  (1,200 clinical hours or master's + 600; chronic-disease Rx; scarce,
  rarely covered), coaches (fitness/wellness/health; NBC-HWC
  standard since 2017 [ref 143]; accessible, variable, private-pay),
  certified personal trainers (18 accrediting bodies; top four: ACE,
  **ACSM** (ACSM-EP gold standard), NASM, NSCA; verify at USREPS
  [ref 144]).
- ACSM certifications (Table 6.9): GEI, CPT, EP, CEP primary;
  Exercise is Medicine credential, inclusive fitness, cancer trainer
  specialty.
- **Cardiopulmonary rehabilitation** [ref 145]: structured
  multidisciplinary program (exercise + nutrition + behavior +
  immunization + risk-factor control); typical 3×/wk × 12 wk = 36
  monitored sessions; goal 30 min moderate aerobic 5 d/wk; benefits:
  ↓mortality, ↓depression, ↑functional capacity.

## Objective 9 — Prescriptions for special populations

- Everyone: 150–300 min/wk moderate + 2 resistance days as able;
  any amount >0 helps (0→15 min/wk gains more than 135→150) [refs
  1, 19, 88, 146].
- **Obesity:** energy-expenditure focus; build toward 250–300 min/wk;
  non-weight-bearing aerobic options (swim, cycle, row); resistance
  may be more appealing initially; pair with reduced intake.
- **Heart disease:** maintain fitness; cardiopulmonary rehab after:
  MI <12 mo, CABG, stable angina, valve repair/replacement,
  angioplasty/stent, transplant, stable chronic HF with reduced EF.
  Beta-blockers blunt HR response; diuretics → dehydration,
  hypokalemia, orthostatic hypotension — counsel on dizziness.
- **Diabetes** [refs 98–99, 147–149]: cardiorespiratory fitness among
  the strongest mortality predictors in T2DM; no more than 2
  consecutive rest days; aerobic + resistance; cut sedentary time
  (multiple bouts aid glycemic control [ref 144 as taught]). ADA 2016
  position statement [ref 149]. **Glucose rules:** pre-exercise
  100–250 mg/dL → go; >250 → test ketones (postpone if present);
  >350 → postpone; <100 → 10–20 g fast carbs first. Insulin: reduce
  rapid/short-acting ~50% around exercise; carry 10–15 g carbs per 30
  min. Complications: peripheral neuropathy — moderate walking OK
  (no ↑ulcers), re-ulceration caution; nephropathy — exercise helps
  even on dialysis; microalbuminuria alone — no restriction;
  retinopathy — avoid breath-holding, overhead lifting, jarring;
  retinopathy/nephropathy — avoid BP spikes (heavy lifts, Valsalva);
  autonomic neuropathy/CV complications — avoid outdoor heat/humidity.
- **Cancer:** combined aerobic+resistance best for QoL; individualize
  to diagnosis/treatment (bone health, cardiotoxicity, neuropathy,
  lymphedema, fall risk, fatigue) [ref 150].
- **Disability:** provider/specialist consult; start supervised
  (PT/OT/CEP); emphasize locomotion, strength, balance.
- **Stroke:** cardiorespiratory training reduces disability
  [ref 151]; tailor to deficits; PT/OT 3–6 mo; assess gait stability
  before treadmill (stationary/recumbent bike alternatives); avoid
  Valsalva during resistance — exhale on exertion.
- Tools: National Physical Activity Plan, Walk with a Doc, Silver
  Sneakers, Exercise is Medicine, ParkRx, ACE library; devices
  (wearables increase participation [ref 152]); gyms, apps, parks.
