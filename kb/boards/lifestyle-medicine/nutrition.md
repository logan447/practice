# LM Boards — Nutrition (Section 5)

Condensed study notes preserving **what the book teaches**. Claims
carry the book's own reference numbers as `[ref N]`; resolve them in
`nutrition-refs.md` (full citations with DOIs). The foundational
background (vocabulary, macronutrients) is flagged by the book as
**not core ABLM/IBLM exam competency**; Objectives 1–10 are.

## Foundational background (not core exam competency)

- **DRI** umbrella (Food & Nutrition Board): **RDA** meets needs of
  97–98% of healthy people; **AI** used when RDA can't be determined;
  **UL** = eat less than; **DV** = FDA label reference (often ≈ RDA/AI)
  [refs 4–8].
- RDA/AI anchors: carbohydrate 130 g/d; fiber **38 g men / 25 g
  women** (14 g per 1,000 kcal); protein **0.8 g/kg** (~56 g men, 46 g
  women ≈ 10–11% of calories); n-6 fat 17/12 g; n-3 fat 1.6/1.1 g
  [ref 13–14].
- **Protein:** all essential amino acids originate in plants/microbes;
  plant and animal proteins both contain all nine EAAs; complementing
  within a meal is unnecessary — variety over time suffices [refs
  15–24]. Plant foods easily exceed 10–11% kcal protein (spinach 50%,
  soybeans 42%, oats 17% of kcal). Protein needs rise for elderly,
  children, pregnancy, athletes [ref 25].
- **Fats:** replace saturated/trans with mono/polyunsaturated; MUFA
  substitution lowers LDL, CVD events, all-cause mortality [ref 34].
  Saturated FA specifics the book names: lauric (coconut — raises HDL
  and LDL, mixed evidence, no weight/satiety benefit [refs 36–40]),
  stearic (all SFAs correlate with CHD; replacement with PUFA/whole
  grain/plant protein → ~6–8% CHD risk reduction [refs 41–42];
  endothelial damage; colorectal-cancer association [ref 43]),
  palmitic (most common; CVD risk, insulin resistance [ref 42]),
  myristic (hypercholesterolemic; CVD, T2DM, mortality [refs 45–47]).
  **Trans fat:** raises CHD risk [ref 48]; FDA removed GRAS status —
  artificial trans fats out of the US food supply (manufacturing ended
  Jan 1, 2021) [refs 49–50]; label can say 0 g at <0.5 g/serving, so
  check for "partially hydrogenated oil."
- **Carbohydrate:** quality over quantity — whole-food carbs
  (vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains) vs refined [refs 53–58].
  **Fiber** (plants only; none in animal foods): insoluble bulks
  stool, feeds SCFA-producing bacteria, protects gut barrier; soluble
  slows digestion, increases insulin sensitivity, lowers cholesterol
  and glucose [refs 59–70]. No UL set.
- **Micronutrients:** whole-food variety beats supplements for balance
  [refs 80–84]; exceptions the book names: **B12 for plant-based
  diets**, prenatal supplements, vitamin D when sun-limited.
  Antioxidant food families: cruciferous (sulforaphane,
  indole-3-carbinols), dark berries (anthocyanins), allium
  (organosulfur), orange/yellow (carotenoids), dark leafy greens.

## Objective 1 — Intake patterns: nutrients of deficit and excess

- **Overconsumed (US):** added sugars (~14.1% of calories; SSBs the
  top source [refs 85–86]; WHO <10% of calories, goal <5%; **AHA ≤100
  kcal/6 tsp/24 g women, ≤150 kcal/9 tsp/36 g men** [refs 87–88]),
  saturated fat (AHA 5–6% of calories [ref 34]; top US sources:
  cheese 16.5%, beef 9.1% [ref 90]), sodium (~70% from
  prepackaged/processed + restaurant food [ref 91]; Novick rule of
  thumb: sodium mg/serving ≤ kcal/serving ≈ ≤2,300 mg/day).
- Special mentions: dietary **cholesterol** (animal foods only; AHA
  steers via overall pattern [ref 92]; top sources meat+eggs+grain
  dishes+dairy ≈ 96%), trans fats, and **Group 1 carcinogens:
  alcoholic beverages and processed meat** [ref 96].
- **Underconsumed:** calcium*, choline, fiber*, magnesium, potassium*,
  vitamins A, C, D*, E, K (* = public-health concern [ref 1]). Food
  sources worth knowing: calcium — low-oxalate greens (kale, collards,
  bok choy; spinach/chard/beet greens are high-oxalate so calcium
  poorly absorbed [refs 98–100]); potassium — potatoes, beans, banana;
  vitamin D — fatty fish, fortified milk; sun 10am–3pm 5–30 min ≥2×/wk
  [ref 106].
- **Nutrient density** ranking (most shortfall nutrients per kcal):
  vegetables > herbs/spices > fruits > legumes > whole grains > nuts >
  seeds [ref 108]. A whole-food plant-predominant day can meet
  everything except vitamin D (and B12 for fully plant-based) [refs
  106, 109].
- Patient-facing principles: judge the whole "food package"; the more
  whole/less processed, the better; "eat food, not too much, mostly
  plants" (Pollan [ref 110]); "eat a rainbow" [refs 111–113].

## Objective 2 — Food labels

- Serving size = basis of everything else; containers often hold
  2–3 servings [ref 116]. %DV rule of thumb: **≤5% low, ≥20% high**
  [ref 126].
- Fiber: DV 28 g/2,000 kcal; Harvard **10:1 carbohydrate-to-fiber
  rule** for whole-grain products (carb g ÷ 10 ≤ fiber g) [refs
  121–122].
- Added sugars line is new; WHO <10% of intake [refs 123–124]. Sodium:
  ≤2,300 mg/d; frozen-dinner heuristic ≤500 mg/serving [ref 1].
- Trans fat: "0 g" allowed under 0.5 g/serving — scan ingredients for
  partially hydrogenated oils [refs 119–120].
- Ingredient list is by descending weight; first three ingredients
  tell the story; "whole" must precede the grain ("multigrain,"
  "stone-ground," "enriched flour" ≠ whole grain) [ref 127].
- Front-of-package: unregulated — "natural," "low sugar"; regulated —
  "good source" (10–19% DV), "excellent source" (≥20% DV),
  "gluten-free" (<20 ppm), "certified organic."

## Objective 3 — Global dietary guideline commonalities

- ACLM meta-epidemiological study (2021–22; 78 guidelines 2010–2021,
  83% from medical societies) [ref 131]: most frequently **encouraged**
  food groups — vegetables 74%, fruit 69%, whole grains 58%,
  legumes 47%, nuts/seeds 44%, low-fat dairy 38%, fish 36%. Most
  **discouraged** — red meat 32%, processed meat 27%, refined grains
  19%, fatty meat 12%. **No guideline encouraged fatty/red meat,
  full-fat dairy, refined grains, or salted nuts.**
- **ACLM dietary position statement** (memorize): *"For the treatment,
  reversal, and prevention of lifestyle-related chronic disease, the
  ACLM recommends eating a plant-predominant diet based on a variety
  of minimally processed vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes,
  nuts, and seeds."* — found to align with global guidelines.
- US DGA 2020–2025: USDA+HHS, 5-year cycle, professional-audience,
  politics can shift final text from the scientific report [refs 1,
  132–133]. Canada's guide: plant-forward, water as beverage of
  choice [ref 134].

## Objective 4 — Dietary patterns and their health impact

- **History:** Burkitt — Western diseases share diet (fiber) etiology
  [refs 135–137]. GBD 2017: poor diet causes more deaths than tobacco
  or high BP; 1 in 5 deaths globally; top factors: high sodium, low
  whole grains, low fruit [ref 108].
- **Western/SAD:** low whole plants; high red/processed meat, trans
  fat, sodium, added sugar; ~60% of energy from **UPFs** [refs
  138–144]. UPF harms: +weight (~500 kcal/d excess in Hall RCT [ref
  150]), obesity, CVD; >4 servings/d → 62% higher all-cause mortality,
  +18% per additional serving [ref 151].
- **Plant-predominant patterns** (vegetarian spectrum: flexitarian →
  pescatarian → lacto-ovo → vegan → whole-food plant-based):
  associated with lower T2DM, IHD, cancer risk [refs 154–155].
  Low-CAD populations: rural Uganda, Tarahumara, Papua highlanders,
  rural China (cholesterol 127 vs 203 mg/dL US; 16.7-fold CAD
  mortality gap) [refs 156–160].
- **Mediterranean:** high MUFA:SFA ratio, legumes, produce, fish;
  moderate wine/dairy; low meat. High adherence → **−41% CVD
  mortality, −27% CVD incidence** [refs 162–163].
- **DASH:** low sat/trans fat, high K/Ca/Mg/fiber/protein, sodium
  1,500–2,300 mg. Lowers LDL, BP, diabetes and CVD mortality [refs
  164–170].
- **Portfolio:** plant sterols + soy protein + viscous fiber + almonds
  → **LDL −28.6%, ≈ lovastatin 20 mg (−30.9%)** [refs 71, 82].
- **MIND** (Mediterranean+DASH, berries + leafy greens, less
  meat/sweets/fried/cheese): Alzheimer's protection even with moderate
  adherence [ref 171].
- Table 5.6 evidence bullets worth retaining: BROAD (WFPB → BMI
  reduction beyond usual care [ref 172]); vegan > omnivorous for
  weight loss (New DIETs [ref 173]); lifestyle change −47% T2DM risk
  (meta-analysis [ref 175]); fiber/whole grains: lower all-cause and
  CV mortality, CHD, stroke, T2DM, colorectal cancer; trials: lower
  weight, SBP, total cholesterol [ref 53]; Adventist: hypertension 7%
  of vegans vs 23% of non-vegetarians [ref 181]; healthful plant-based
  index ↓T2DM via weight [ref 178]; MIND/DASH/Mediterranean adherence
  ↓Alzheimer's [ref 171]; vegetarian ↓IHD incidence+mortality and
  total cancer; vegan ↓total cancer [ref 189].

## Objective 5 — Processing level and health

- **NOVA 4 categories:** (1) un/minimally processed; (2) processed
  culinary ingredients (butter, salt, sugar, oils); (3) processed
  (bacon, cheeses w/ salt, canned-in-syrup, fresh bread); (4) **UPFs**
  (industrial formulations: colors, flavorings, emulsifiers,
  preservatives + added fat/salt/sweeteners) [refs 144, 222].
- Processing can help (pasteurization, freezing, fortification: iron/B
  vitamins in infant cereal, folic-acid flour → fewer NTDs, calcium-
  fortified OJ) [refs 192–195] and harm (refining strips bran/germ;
  drying loses vitamin C/B; peeling discards the nutrient-rich outer
  portion) [refs 196–198].
- Undesirable additions: artificial dyes (hyperactivity; thyroid-tumor
  signal in animals [refs 199–200]), sodium nitrite (cured meat —
  cancer risk [refs 201–202]), artificial sweeteners, added sugar,
  saturated/trans fats, salt (~70% of US sodium from prepackaged/
  processed foods [ref 207]).
- UPF hyperpalatability is engineered (sugar-salt-fat combinations)
  [ref 223]; UPFs >50% of US calories, ~90% of added-sugar intake
  [refs 224–225].
- Cooking: water-soluble vitamins (C, B) leach/degrade with boiling
  and high heat; steaming/roasting lose less than boiling; frying
  degrades omega-3s and generates aldehydes (CVD/cancer-associated)
  [refs 212–219]; grilling/broiling form **PAHs** [refs 220–221].
- **AGEs** (glycotoxins): formed by dry/high-heat cooking of fatty
  animal foods; accumulate with age/hyperglycemia; oxidative stress,
  inflammation, diabetes, Alzheimer's, heart/liver disease [refs
  228–233]. Highest: broiling 225°C, frying; fried bacon highest of
  549 foods. **Lower AGEs: moist, lower-temperature, shorter cooking;
  acidic marinades (vinegar, lemon, tomato); stewing, poaching,
  steaming, boiling** [ref 228].

## Objective 6 — Culinary medicine

- CM = evidence-based blend of nutrition + culinary science to help
  patients maintain health and prevent/treat disease; in LM, aimed at
  implementing a whole-food plant-predominant pattern.
- Why: poor diet is the #1 contributor to early US death, yet only
  ~25% of medical schools have nutrition courses [refs 233–235];
  provider self-efficacy predicts use of nutrition prescriptions
  [ref 236].
- **ACLM Culinary Medicine Curriculum (CMC):** free, downloadable,
  by Dr. Michelle Hauser (Stanford); 9 sessions (knife skills →
  sauté/stir-fry → roasting → soups/salads → beans & bowls →
  breakfasts → pastas → dessert flip → potluck); teaches food swaps,
  mindful eating, herbs/spices over salt, budget strategies.

## Objective 7 — Landmark nutrition studies (prevention)

Theme: patterns higher in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes,
fiber lower chronic-disease risk.

- **NHS** (1976–; 3 cohorts, >230k women; Willett FFQ) [refs 237–239]:
  Mediterranean pattern ↓CHD/stroke; refined carbs + trans ↑CHD;
  ≥1 drink/day and red meat ↑breast-cancer risk; folate/B6/Ca/vit D
  ↓colon cancer.
- **HPFS** (1986–, 51,529 men) [ref 240]: SSBs ↑T2DM/CHD/mortality
  (≥2/day → +21% overall, +31% CVD death [ref 242]); red meat ↑CHD
  11–15%, plant-protein substitution ↓14–17% [ref 243]; olive oil
  ≥1.5 tsp/d ↓CVD 14% [ref 244].
- **WHI** (161k women) [refs 247–260]: HT did not prevent CHD; DM
  trial's modest fat reduction (goal 20%, achieved 24% year 1) did not
  reduce breast/colorectal cancer or CVD at 8 years; CaD → modest hip
  bone density gain, no fracture/cancer reduction.
- **EPIC-Oxford** (65k, heavy vegetarian representation) [refs
  261–267]: vegans — lowest BMI (~22.5), highest fiber, lowest
  SFA (5% of energy); combined vegetarian+vegan ↓diabetes 37% (vegan
  alone 47%); vegetarians ↓IHD 22% (but +20% stroke); vegetarians+
  vegans ↓total cancer 12%.
- **Adventist Health Studies** [refs 268–276]: legumes ↓colon/
  prostate/pancreatic cancer; meat ↑colon/bladder/ovarian; nuts ≥5×/wk
  ↓nonfatal MI ~74% (AHS-2: 1–4×/wk) and ↓fatal CHD ~73%; vegans ~30 lb
  lighter than non-vegetarians at same height; T2DM prevalence in
  vegans/lacto-ovo ≈ half of non-vegetarians.
- **Framingham** [refs 277–281]: BP ≥120–139/80–89 already raises CVD
  risk; SBP predicts stroke/HF more than DBP.
- **MESA** [refs 282–285]: tomatoes/beans/refined grains/high-fat
  dairy/red meat pattern +18% T2DM; whole grains/fruit/nuts/greens/
  low-fat dairy −15%.
- **NHANES** [refs 286–292]: UPFs >50% of calories; every +5% calories
  from UPFs → −0.14 points cardiovascular-health score.

**Treatment/reversal RCTs:**

- **Lyon Diet Heart** [refs 293–295]: post-MI Mediterranean (ALA-rich
  canola-oil margarine) — stopped early; 50–70% lower recurrent
  cardiac events; benefit independent of lipids/BP.
- **OmniHeart** [refs 296–298]: replacing carbohydrate with protein or
  unsaturated fat further lowers BP, lipids, 10-yr risk vs
  carb-rich DASH-style diet.
- **DASH trial** (1997, N=459) + 30-RCT meta-analysis [refs 299–301]:
  combination diet lowers SBP −3.2 / DBP −2.5 mmHg overall (larger
  with higher sodium, younger age; classic trial: ~−11 mmHg SBP in
  hypertensives — see Objective 9).
- **PREDIMED** [refs 302–303]: high-CV-risk primary prevention;
  Mediterranean + EVOO or nuts ↓major CV events vs low-fat control;
  pro-vegetarian variant ↓all-cause mortality.

## Objective 8 — Mechanisms: how nutrition addresses chronic disease

- **Inflammation** [refs 304–328]: Western patterns (red/processed
  meat, refined flour, sugar, sat/trans fat) ↑; Mediterranean/DASH/
  fruit-vegetable patterns ↓. "Functional foods" with trial-level
  anti-inflammatory signal: cranberries, grapes, pomegranate,
  strawberries, whole grains, low-fat dairy, yogurt, green tea,
  cardamom, turmeric, soy, almonds, chia, flaxseed, pistachios, algae
  oil, grape-seed oil [ref 322]. Antioxidant-rich patterns associate
  with longer telomeres [ref 327]. Dairy's inflammatory role:
  unresolved [refs 323–326].
- **Oxidation** [refs 329–335]: plant-predominant patterns
  (Mediterranean, DASH, vegetarian, HEI, paleo in observational data)
  ↓oxidative-stress biomarkers; Western/fast-food ↑. Repeated
  high-heat frying oils → cytotoxic lipid-oxidation products.
  Whole-food antioxidants work; **antioxidant supplements do not
  reduce cancer risk — whole foods do** [refs 410, 110].
- **Glycosylation** [refs 336–339]: vegan diet RCT improved IgG
  glycosylation/inflammatory activity in RA vs meat-rich diet
  [ref 337].
- **Epigenetics** [refs 340–345]: diet-induced modifications are
  reversible; harmful — red/processed meat, sweetened beverages,
  alcohol, sedentary behavior; beneficial — legumes, nuts, fruit,
  seaweed, dairy-lower, plant-rich patterns (vegan/vegetarian/DASH/
  Mediterranean); anti-inflammatory diet ↔ slower biological aging
  [ref 343]; NU-AGE tailored Mediterranean → epigenetic rejuvenation
  [ref 344].
- **Microbiome** [refs 308, 346–352]: ~90% Bacteroidetes/Firmicutes;
  diet shifts microbiota in real time; Mediterranean adherence ↔ lower
  Parkinson's/Alzheimer's risk (causality unproven [ref 348]); almonds
  ↑SCFA producers [ref 349]; probiotic adjuncts improved weight-loss
  outcomes in an 11-RCT review [ref 350]; direction of diet-microbiome-
  disease causality still open [ref 351].
- **Prescription themes:** center whole plant foods; cut animal,
  ultra-processed, fried, energy-dense foods; antioxidant-rich
  (darkly colored) foods for oncology; most patients respond to
  simple food-based prescriptions over mechanism talk.

## Objective 9 — Writing nutrition prescriptions

- **USPSTF:** intensive behavioral diet+activity counseling —
  **Grade B** with overweight/chronic-disease risk factors; **Grade C**
  (selective, readiness-based) without risk factors [refs 353–356].
  Physician PA-prescription precedent: +131 min activity vs +18 min
  with advice alone [ref 357].
- Foundation diet: whole-food plant-predominant, high fiber and
  antioxidants, low total/saturated fat, added sugar, added salt.
- **Format — "TAF" (opposite of FAT):** **T**ype of food (specific),
  **A**mount (exact), **F**requency (may be time-limited), plus
  patient name, date, signature. Positive scripts ("eat more/swap")
  are better accepted than negative ("consume less"); SMART-goal
  format also works.
- **Disease-specific targets:**
  - **Hypertension:** ↓sodium (UL 2,300 mg; AHA ideal <1,500 mg;
    salt-sensitivity highest in Black and ≥65 patients [refs
    389–391]), ↓alcohol (J-curve [refs 392–393]), ↓caffeine;
    ↑potassium (potatoes, fruit, greens, legumes), ↑calcium
    (low-oxalate greens, beans, fortified non-dairy milk; low-fat
    dairy per DASH −11 mmHg SBP over 8 wk [refs 394–395]), ↑magnesium
    (nuts, beans, avocado), garlic [ref 396]; medically supervised
    water-only fasting exists in the book [ref 397].
  - **Hyperlipidemia:** ↓trans/saturated fat, processed foods, total
    meat; ↑PUFAs, complex carbs, plant sterols/stanols, viscous fiber
    (oatmeal, soybeans, okra, eggplant — Portfolio logic) [refs
    398–399].
  - **Diabetes:** ↓dietary fat, processed sugars/grains; ↑fiber; use
    **GL over GI** (GL = GI × carb intake [ref 403]): watermelon GI 76
    but GL 8; doughnut GI 76, GL 17; cooled potatoes (resistant
    starch) drop GI ~40%; GI/GL ≠ healthfulness (fat lowers GI).
  - **Cancer:** ↓pro-inflammatory foods (sat fat, processed sugar,
    red/processed meat), protein moderation at younger ages [refs
    406–407]; ↑plant:animal protein ratio, fiber 30–35 g, antioxidant
    foods (β-carotene, lycopene, resveratrol, selenium, C, E) — foods,
    not supplements [refs 89, 410]. Site-specifics: breast — obesity,
    alcohol, meat ↑risk; soy/fruit/vegetables ↓ [refs 255, 411–414];
    prostate — lycopene, selenium, whole soy ↓ [refs 415–418]; colon —
    red/processed meat ↑; fiber, Ca, folate ↓ [refs 419–420]; gastric —
    processed meat ↑, produce ↓ [refs 421–422].
- Practice pointers: start with what to **add**; match to stage of
  change; be specific; measurable goals; offer a pick-list of habits;
  food/exercise journal; mindful-eating and CBT techniques; team-based
  long-term support.

## Objective 10 — Referral to the registered dietitian

- Provider role: baseline H&P, risk factors, frame nutrition as core
  treatment, set goals, **oversee RD recommendations**.
- **Refer when:** complex chronic conditions (diabetes, renal disease,
  CVD, weight disorders, HIV/cancer, liver/GI disease, celiac,
  Crohn's, malnutrition, malabsorption, bariatric surgery, eating
  disorders, food allergies, pregnancy weight concerns); liquid meals;
  weight-enhancing medications; not responding to nutrition
  interventions [ref 427].
- RD role: assess/monitor status, individualize, coach, track
  biomarker impact, provide **medical nutrition therapy** [refs
  428–431].
- **PES statement** (nutrition diagnosis): Problem *related to*
  Etiology *as evidenced by* Signs/symptoms.
- **ABCD assessment:** **A**nthropometric (wt, ht, BMI, waist),
  **B**iochemical (Na, K, Hgb/Hct, glucose, HbA1c, albumin, lipids,
  Ca, vit D, B12, iron, ferritin), **C**linical (history, social,
  activity, weight/nutrition history, functional ability, budget,
  readiness), **D**ietary (intake assessment; tools in Section 3).
  Practical tip: food models/portion examples help.
