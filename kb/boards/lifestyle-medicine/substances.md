# LM Boards — Substance Use (Section 9)

## Objective 1 — Health effects of tobacco, alcohol, other substances

- **Tobacco:** >$300B/yr ($225B direct medical + $156B productivity)
  [ref 1]; single largest preventable cause of US morbidity/
  mortality [ref 2]; 34M US smokers (~14% of adults) [ref 3]; 8M
  global deaths/yr incl. secondhand; >80% of users in low/middle-
  income countries [ref 4]; **480,000 US deaths/yr** (~1 in 5),
  kills 1 of every 2 users [refs 4–5]; life expectancy ≥10 y shorter
  [ref 2]. Mortality drivers: cancer 163,700 > CVD 153,000 >
  respiratory 113,100 [ref 2]. Smoking explains ~90% of lung-cancer
  risk in men, 70–80% in women (adenocarcinoma now most common
  smoking-associated type) [refs 6–7]; without tobacco, 1 in 3
  cancer deaths wouldn't happen [ref 8]. Other risks [refs 9–11]:
  diabetes (+30–40% in current smokers), RA, immune impairment,
  reproduction problems, wound healing, TB, ectopic pregnancy/
  preterm/stillbirth/LBW/SIDS, cleft lip/palate, ED, macular
  degeneration, all-cancer treatment failure, fractures.
- **Nicotine dependence** [refs 3, 12]: most common chemical
  dependence in the US; on par with cocaine/heroin/alcohol for
  cessation difficulty; 70% of smokers want to quit; **average of
  six quit attempts** before long-term abstinence.
- **Alcohol:** 22% binge drank in the past month; 6.4% heavy use
  [refs 13–14]; of 40.3M with SUD in 2020, 28.3M (14.54% of 12+) had
  AUD [ref 14]; **>140,000 deaths/yr, 3.6M years of potential life
  lost** [ref 16]; acute events (poisonings, crashes, homicides,
  suicides) >58,000 deaths/yr [ref 17]; **HHS-listed carcinogen** —
  clearest cancer links: head & neck, esophagus, liver, breast (F),
  colon/rectum; dose-dependent; additive with tobacco [refs 18–21];
  alcohol-related deaths +49% 2006→2019 [ref 22]; raises BP, stroke,
  injuries, obstetric complications/FASD. Red-wine phenols
  (resveratrol, quercetin, catechin) may benefit but must be weighed
  against known risks; **USDA DGA 2020–2025 does not recommend
  alcohol** [refs 23–24]. Binge pattern most common (31.4% of 18–25;
  rises with income; White > Black/Asian; M 2× F) [ref 25]. Misuse
  risk factors: other substances, psychiatric comorbidity, trauma
  history, family history.
- **Illicit drugs** [ref 14]: 21.4% of 12+ used in 2020 (59.3M);
  marijuana most common (17.9%; highest 18–25), then nonmedical
  prescription pain relievers; opioids — 9.5M misusers (2.4%);
  overdose deaths (Rx/heroin/synthetic) up >6× since 1999, majority
  of ~50,000 involve synthetics (fentanyl); synthetic-involved death
  rate +1,040% 2013–2019 [refs 26–27]; OUD economic impact $471B +
  $550B fatal overdose (2017) [ref 28].

## Objective 2 — Screening tools

- **Tobacco:** USPHS guideline — tobacco dependence is a **chronic
  disease**; assess and document use at every visit; treatments
  highly cost-effective; **brief (<3 min) interventions work**;
  dose-response for counseling intensity (>4 person-to-person
  sessions especially effective); unwilling quitters → MI
  [refs 29–30].
- **Lung-cancer screening (USPSTF 2021):** annual low-dose CT if
  **≥20 pack-years + current smoker or quit <15 y + age 50–80**
  [refs 31–32].
- Nicotine-dependence scales: **Fagerström** (10 items; 7–10 high,
  4–6 moderate, <4 minimal) [ref 33]; **Heaviness of Smoking Index**
  (2 items: time-to-first-cigarette, cigarettes/day; 0–1 low, 2–4
  medium, 5–6 high) [ref 34]; Minnesota Tobacco Withdrawal
  Scale-Revised; Hughes-Hatsukami self-report [refs 35–37].
- **Alcohol:** US dietary guidance limits — ≤2 drinks/day men, ≤1
  women; standard drink = **14 g ethanol** (12 oz 5% beer, 5 oz 12%
  wine, 1.5 oz 80-proof); container math: 750 mL wine = 5 drinks,
  fifth = 17, 1 L = 22, handle = 39 [ref 38]. Definitions: binge
  (BAC 0.08 — ~5 M/4 F drinks in 2 h); heavy use (binge ≥5 days/
  month; NIAAA >4/day M, >3/day F); at-risk; AUD (≥2 of 11 DSM
  criteria) [ref 39].
- **USPSTF: screen all adults ≥18 for unhealthy alcohol use in
  primary care** [ref 39]. Tools [ref 40]: CAGE (dependence-
  oriented), **AUDIT/AUDIT-C**, NMASSIST, CRAFFT (ages 12–21), TAPS,
  MSASQ. NIAAA single-item: "How many times in the past year have
  you had ≥5 (M)/≥4 (F) drinks in a day?" — ≥1 → document frequency/
  quantity, advise cut-offs (M >4/day or >14/wk; F >3/day or >7/wk),
  written screen [ref 41]. **AUDIT scoring** [refs 42–43]: 0–6/7
  feedback; 7/8–15 brief intervention; 16–24 + monitoring/outpatient;
  **≥25 refer for evaluation and treatment** (= SBIRT: screen, brief
  intervention, referral to treatment).
- Regular drinkers: also screen BP, cognition/cardiac/liver exam,
  nutrition + osteoporosis risk, labs (AST/ALT, CBC) [refs 45–47].
- **Drugs:** USPSTF — ask adults ≥18 about unhealthy drug use (when
  services can be offered) [ref 48]; **TAPS** (self- or clinician-
  administered; TAPS-1 four questions, any non-"never" positive →
  TAPS-2; NIDA: ≥2 → >70% sensitivity for SUD) [refs 49–51];
  NMASSIST, PRO. Known use → end-organ-directed exam; IV → abscess/
  endocarditis/HIV/hepatitis; inhalation → respiratory; risky sexual
  behavior → STI/hepatitis/pregnancy/HIV testing [ref 51].

## Objective 3 — DSM-5-TR diagnostic criteria

- "Use disorder" = problematic pattern → clinically significant
  impairment [refs 52–53]. **11 criteria**, four clusters: impaired
  control (larger/longer than intended; unsuccessful cut-down;
  excessive time obtaining/using/recovering; craving), social
  impairment (role failure; continued use despite interpersonal
  problems; activities given up), risky use (hazardous situations;
  continued despite physical/psychological harm), pharmacologic
  (tolerance; withdrawal — alcohol: tachycardia, HTN, tremor,
  altered sensorium/perception; opioid: anxiety, N/V/D, bone/joint
  aches, pupillary dilation).
- Severity (all but tobacco): **mild 2–3, moderate 4–5, severe ≥6**;
  remission = criteria no longer met (early ≥3 mo; sustained ≥12
  mo). Volume/frequency alone do not define a disorder.

## Objective 4 — Treatment plans (pharmacotherapy)

- **Tobacco:** encourage medication for all quitters **except**
  where contraindicated or insufficient evidence: pregnancy,
  smokeless users, light smokers (<10 CPD — no significant NRT
  benefit), adolescents [ref 29].
- **Seven FDA first-line medications** (Table 9.1) [refs 53–56]:
  - **Patch** (24-h; OTC): >40 CPD 42 mg/d, 21–39 CPD 21–35, 10–20
    CPD 14–21, <10 CPD 14; taper every 2–4 wk by 7–14 mg after 4–6
    wk abstinence; nighttime use can cut morning cravings.
  - **Lozenge** (OTC; 2/4 mg): time-to-first-cigarette <30 min → 4
    mg; >20 CPD → 4 mg; 1–2 q1–2h; 25% better absorption than gum;
    no eating/drinking 15 min prior.
  - **Gum** (OTC; 2/4 mg; same selection rules): "chew and park";
    avoid with dental problems.
  - **Nasal spray** (Rx): fastest NRT; 1 spray/nostril 1–2×/h.
  - **Inhaler** (Rx): "puff," don't inhale; 6–16 cartridges/day;
    mimics hand-to-mouth.
  - **Bupropion SR** (Rx): start 1 wk pre-quit; 150 mg daily ×3 d
    then BID; 12 wk–12 mo; lowers seizure threshold; avoid in
    seizure/eating disorders, MAOI use; combinable with NRT; no
    taper needed.
  - **Varenicline** (Rx): start 1 wk pre-quit; 0.5 mg daily ×3 d,
    0.5 BID ×4 d, then 1 mg BID ×11 wk (+12 wk if abstinent); take
    with food; partial nicotinic agonist (prevents withdrawal +
    blocks nicotine highs) [ref 61]; renally metabolized; mood/SI
    screening advised (as taught).
- Mechanism: nicotinic receptors in ventral tegmental area →
  dopamine release [ref 57]. Combination beats monotherapy: patch +
  oral NRT **+34–54% quit rates vs patch alone** [refs 59–60];
  varenicline + patch and varenicline + bupropion show added benefit
  in heavier/dependent smokers [refs 62, 65–66]. Bupropion
  dose-response at 7 wk: 19.0% placebo → 44.2% (300 mg) [refs
  61, 63]. **EAGLES** (n=8,144): varenicline 21.8% 6-mo quit >
  bupropion 16.2% ≈ patch 15.7% > placebo 9.4%; all beat placebo
  [ref 72]. Second-line (off-label): clonidine, nortriptyline.
  Pharmacotherapy + behavior modification = highest quit rate.
- Prescribing factors (Table 9.2): patch — skin disorders/adhesive
  allergy; gum — TMJ, dental work, ulcers; lozenge — ulcers; spray/
  inhaler — reactive airway disease; bupropion — MAOI, seizure,
  bulimia/anorexia, abrupt EtOH/sedative withdrawal (pregnancy C);
  varenicline — serious hypersensitivity history; caution seizure,
  renal impairment, heavy machinery, alcohol tolerance (pregnancy
  C). Weight-gain worry: average gain **<10 lb (4.5 kg)**, more in
  women; bupropion/NRT combo delays but doesn't prevent.
- **Special populations:** CVD — patch not associated with acute CV
  events even in continued smokers; varenicline and combo NRT first
  line [refs 54, 73]; surgical — NRT safe, underprescribed (18.6%),
  teachable moment [refs 74–75]; **pregnancy — counseling is best**;
  OTC NRT benefits outweigh risks (limited research); bupropion/
  varenicline category C, Rx NRT category D; continued smoking
  likely riskier than NRT [refs 54, 76–77]; **adolescents — CBT
  first line; NRT safe but little research**; relapse-prone —
  long-term NRT no known health risk, bupropion ≤6 mo, varenicline
  +12 wk [ref 54].
- **E-cigarettes (ENDS)** [refs 78–80]: 2021 — first FDA marketing
  authorizations; chamber liquid can hold nicotine/THC/other;
  nicotine liquid poisonous if swallowed/absorbed; aerosol has fewer
  chemicals than smoke (7,000+) but carries nickel/tin/lead +
  carcinogens; **EVALI** — most cases involved vaping THC, possibly
  vitamin E acetate; youth: 1/35 middle-school, 1/9 high-school
  past-30-day use; may equal/beat NRT short-term with behavioral
  support, not long-term; too heterogeneous to recommend
  specifically [ref 81].
- **Not evidence-based** [ref 29]: acupuncture, hypnosis
  (ineffective); biofeedback, restricted environmental stimulation,
  incentives (insufficient); unproven methods aren't justified by
  failed conventional attempts.
- **Counseling for cessation:** practical counseling (problem-
  solving/skills training): recognize behaviors and patterns;
  avoid/manage high-risk situations (alcohol, stress, social
  settings, smokers at home); identify triggers (stress, driving,
  coffee, meals, purchase locations); address barriers (education,
  finances, support); coping skills (breathing, affirmations,
  visualization, the **3 D's: delay, drink water, do something
  else**, non-smoker identity, altered social contacts); basic quit
  information; intra-treatment support; **follow-up schedule: 2 wk
  before quit date, 1 wk after, 3 mo, 6 mo**; relapse-prevention
  discussion.
- **Alcohol/other substances:** intensity scales with severity; no
  intervention mandates; treat comorbid depression/anxiety; OARS
  framework (Open-ended questions, Affirmations, Reflections,
  Summarize) [ref 82]; mutual aid (AA, SMART Recovery, Women in
  Sobriety, Moderation Management, Celebration/Dharma Recovery);
  therapy: MI, CBT, DBT (dual-diagnosis skilled practitioners).
- **AUD medications** [refs 83–87]: **naltrexone 50 mg PO daily**
  (mu-opioid blocker; modest improvements in relapse, drinking days,
  craving; usable during abstinence or active use);
  **acamprosate** (glutamate modulation; second-line due to TID
  dosing; best in abstinence); **disulfiram 250 mg** (aldehyde
  dehydrogenase inhibitor — acetaldehyde reaction; high
  non-compliance, no better than placebo); off-label: gabapentin
  300–600 mg BID-TID (abstinence 11–17% vs 4% placebo; misuse
  concern), topiramate 100–150 mg BID (reduces heavy-drinking days
  ~8.4%; paresthesia/concentration limits).
- **OUD medications** [ref 88]: naltrexone PO 50 mg → **380 mg IM
  q4wk** (start 7–14 d after last opioid); **buprenorphine** (partial
  agonist, high affinity; precipitated withdrawal risk; SL tabs/
  films ± naloxone, ER injectable); **methadone** (full agonist,
  long half-life; OUD use only via opioid treatment programs;
  take-homes up to a month with protocol adherence).
- **Refer to addiction specialist** (addiction medicine/psychiatry):
  life impairment, unable to make lifestyle changes, failed initial
  interventions, moderation attempts unsuccessful, complicated
  psychiatric/physical comorbidity, significant withdrawal concern,
  higher-than-outpatient level of care.

## Objective 5 — Behavior therapy + pharmacotherapy (5 A's, MI)

- **5 A's of tobacco cessation** [refs 29, 89–90]: **Ask** (document
  every patient, every visit — tobacco as vital sign); **Advise**
  (clear, strong, personalized: "no safe level"); **Assess**
  (willingness to quit; willing → assist; unwilling → motivational
  intervention); **Assist** (quit plan with date within 2 wk;
  medications; counseling; remove tobacco from environment;
  anticipate triggers — alcohol, other smokers, withdrawal,
  high-risk times; quit lines 1-800-QUIT-NOW); **Arrange**
  (follow-up within first week post-quit, again within first month,
  then 3/6 mo). Behavior-change variant: Assess, Advise, **Agree**,
  Assist, Arrange. Time-constrained: **Ask, Advise, Refer**.
- **MI principles** (Table 9.5): express empathy, develop
  discrepancy, roll with resistance, support self-efficacy.
  **5 R's** for the unmotivated (Table 9.6): Relevance, Risks,
  Rewards, Roadblocks, Repetition (every visit).

## Objective 6 — USPSTF recommendations (substances)

- **Tobacco (2021):** Grade A — ask all adults; behavioral
  interventions + FDA pharmacotherapy for non-pregnant adults;
  pregnant persons — ask/advise + behavioral interventions
  (insufficient evidence for pharmacotherapy and e-cigarettes in
  pregnancy) [ref 90].
- **Drug use (2020):** Grade B — ask adults ≥18 about unhealthy drug
  use when diagnosis/treatment/care can be offered or referred
  [refs 91–93].
- **Alcohol:** Grade B — screen adults ≥18 (incl. pregnant) in
  primary care + brief behavioral counseling for risky drinkers;
  insufficient evidence ages 12–17 [ref 92].
