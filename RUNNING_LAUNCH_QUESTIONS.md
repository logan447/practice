## PSLF verification for the 30-hour role (checked 2026-10-24 — VERIFIED from primary text)

- **Full-time = 30 hr/wk, three alternative routes.** 34 CFR
  685.219(b)(11) (current text, Cornell eCFR mirror):
  (A) "A minimum average of 30 hours per week during the period being
  certified"; (B) "A minimum of 30 hours per week throughout a
  contractual or employment period of at least 8 months in a 12-month
  period ... in which case the borrower is deemed to have worked full
  time" — the "such as" teachers/professors clause is illustrative, not
  limiting; (C) credit-hour equivalence (higher ed only).
- **Employer-provided PAID leave counts toward the hours average.**
  Current PSLF form (pulled from studentaid.gov 2026-10-24, 16 pp.),
  Section 7 definitions: "For PSLF purposes, full-time means at least
  an average of 30 hours per week for the employment period being
  certified. All hours for which you are paid should be included in
  the average hours worked per week ... Vacation or leave time
  provided by the employer or leave taken for a condition that is a
  qualifying reason for leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act
  of 1993 ... should be included in the average hours worked per
  week." The employer instructions repeat it: "an employer must
  include all hours, including vacation, leave time..." **"All hours
  for which you are paid" is the operative phrase: the 12 weeks must
  be PAID leave under a year-round salary, not unpaid time off.** The
  8-month route is not teachers-only on the form either: "If you are a
  teacher or in another position under contract for at least eight out
  of 12 months..."
- **DO THE ARITHMETIC, it is the whole question.** The test is an
  average over the certified period, i.e. 52 weeks. 40 weeks × 30 hr =
  1,200 paid hours ÷ 52 = **23 hr/wk = PART-TIME = no PSLF.** It only
  reaches 30 if the 12 leave weeks are themselves PAID at 30 hr:
  1,200 worked + 360 paid leave = 1,560 ÷ 52 = **exactly 30.0**.
- **So the contract wording is the control, not the calendar.** It must
  read "30 hours per week, year-round, with 12 weeks of paid leave"
  (1,560 paid hours). If FCHC writes "30 hours a week, 40 weeks a
  year" or "1,200 hours," a careful certifier enters 23 in box 6 and he
  is part-time for ten years. Payroll has to carry the leave as paid
  time, not smooth a 1,200-hour salary over 12 months.
- **Route B (8-month contract) is NOT a usable backstop here.**
  Corrected 2026-10-24: it covers a contract shorter than 12 months
  where the employer still credits a full year (the school-year case).
  A year-round contract with five scattered leave blocks falls under
  the average test instead. Do not plan on it.
- **What the 32-hour buffer does and does not do.** At 32 contracted
  hours, 1,664 ÷ 52 = 32, which survives short weeks and even one
  unpaid 3-week block (30.2). It does NOT survive unpaid leave across
  the board: 32 × 40 ÷ 52 = 24.6. The buffer covers sloppiness, not a
  wrongly written contract.
- **Religious-activity exclusion: REPEALED, do not plan around it.**
  That sentence still printed on older copies of the certification form
  ("do not include any hours you spent on religious instruction,
  worship services, or proselytizing") reflects the pre-2021 rule. The
  2020 amendment (85 FR 49798, 8/14/2020, effective 7/1/2021) removed
  it; the current 34 CFR 685.219 contains no religious carve-out at
  all. The 10/31/2025 final rule (90 FR, effective 7/1/2026) recites
  the history: in 2020 the definition was changed "to allow employees
  of organizations engaged in religious activities (regardless of
  whether the borrower's duties included religious instruction, worship
  services, or any form of proselytizing) to be eligible for PSLF."
  VERIFIED from govinfo text of the 2025 rule. Operational risk is not
  the law but the certifier: if FCHC fills out a stale copy of the form
  and subtracts devotional or prayer hours, the certification
  understates the week. Use the current form from studentaid.gov and
  have them certify all 30 hours. CONFIRMED by pulling the current
  form: "religious" and "proselytizing" appear ZERO times in its
  16 pages. Only older copies carry that sentence.
- **Contract 32 hr/wk, not 30.** At exactly 30 a single short week can
  pull the certified average under the line. 32 costs FCHC nothing in
  the proposal's framing and removes the risk.
- **Salary is irrelevant to qualification.** $65k vs $80k changes
  nothing about eligibility; it lowers the income-driven payment, so
  more is forgiven. EST: the real cost of the $15k cut is ~$9-10k/yr
  after tax and lower loan payments. NEEDS-CPA.

## Funding a FOR-PROFIT PLLC to serve underserved patients (researched 2026-08-27)

**No grant writes a check to a PLLC for charity care.** Four real
mechanisms exist instead:
1. **NHSC-approved site** — VERIFIED that for-profit "private
   practices (solo or group)" ARE an eligible site type. But
   requirements rebuild the practice: accept Medicare *assignment*,
   maintain Medicaid/CHIP agreements, sliding fee discount program
   (free/nominal <=100% FPL, discounted 100-200% FPL), "no one denied
   for inability to pay" signage, HPSA location. **Explicitly
   ineligible: 100%-telehealth facilities and home-based settings of
   patients or clinicians.** Incompatible with the current
   telemedicine-predominant, no-office, no-insurance design. Real
   option only if he builds a different practice.
2. **Cross-subsidy inside the practice** — no grant, no eligibility
   gate, fully under his control. Precedents: Unorthodoc Foundation's
   Pillar/Core/Arch tiers; East Bay Health (members fund care for the
   uninsured). NOTE: reopens D-029 (single flat published $100, no
   tiers) but serves the already-open D-003/X-10 affordability item.
3. **Own 501(c)(3)** raising charity funds that sponsor memberships at
   the PLLC — legal but needs independent board, nonprofit control of
   the program, FMV payments to the PLLC, no private benefit /
   inurement. IRS red line: must "look nothing like an unprofitable
   private practice dressed in charitable language." ~12+ mo to
   determination, real ongoing admin.
4. **Fiscal sponsorship** by an existing 501(c)(3) — faster than #3;
   sponsor must control the charitable activity; typical 5-15% admin
   fee.
Also: direct sponsorship by churches/employers/individuals is revenue,
not philanthropy — legal and immediate, just not deductible unless
routed through a nonprofit. Foundation grants to for-profits are
technically possible (expenditure responsibility / PRIs) but rare and
burdensome — not worth pursuing. SBIR/STTR requires for-profit but
funds R&D, not care delivery.
**Checked and ruled out as funders:** Unorthodoc Foundation (operates
its own clinics, does not fund outside practices); DirectMD Foundation
(tiny, no published outside-practice program).
**Strategic implication:** FCHC already IS the nonprofit with the
sliding scale, charitable mission, and donor base. Plugging into it
beats rebuilding that infrastructure — which is what the existing
two-settings architecture already does.

### Hourly vs salaried: CORRECTED 2026-10-25

An earlier version of this section said hourly pay disqualifies.
That was wrong. The operative text (FACT, sourced, 34 CFR
685.219(b)(11)(i)(A)) is:

> "A minimum average of 30 hours per week during the period being
> certified"

That is the whole test. The pre-2022 rule required the GREATER of 30
hours or the employer's own definition of full-time; that comparison
is **gone** from the current regulation. So FCHC does not have to
call the role full-time, pay does not have to be salaried, and the
word "leave" need not appear. Total paid hours divided by weeks in
the period must be at least 30.

Salary is therefore not a PSLF requirement. It is a way to be PAID
for 1,560 hours while being PRESENT for 1,200. What hourly costs,
at 1,560 hours with no paid leave (DERIVED):

| days/wk | hrs/day | clinic days | wks worked | wks off | away/day |
|---------|---------|-------------|-----------|---------|----------|
| 3 | 10 | 156 | 52.0 | 0    | 12.5 h |
| 3 | 11 | 142 | 47.3 | 4.7  | 13.5 h |
| 3 | 12 | 130 | 43.3 | 8.7  | 14.5 h |
| 3 | 13 | 120 | 40.0 | 12   | 15.5 h |
| 4 | 10 | 156 | 39.0 | 13   | 12.5 h |

Every hourly row breaks one of the seven constraints: 13-hr days
recreate the 6am-9:30pm day already rejected, 12-hr days cost 3.3
weeks of leave, and 4 days/week breaks the 3-day career rule.
Priced at $80K: hourly 3x12 saves $23,765 with 8.7 weeks off;
salaried 0.8 FTE saves $26,459 with 12. Salary buys 3.3 weeks,
2.5 hrs/day and $2,694.

Remaining argument for salary is administrative, not legal: hourly
leaves the denominator in FCHC's hands. Cancelled clinics shrink
the average and it shows up only at certification. A stated FTE
fixes the number in advance.

operative standard and the owner has the field text by screenshot.
