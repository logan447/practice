# Outreach Packet — Architecture (Proposal)

*Track B artifact. Status: **proposal for review** — no copy is drafted
until this architecture is approved. Distribution is an Era-2
(launch-runway) act: nothing here is printed or handed out until the
publishing gates clear (name/entity/licensure, and the TMB
advertising/counsel pass, `NEEDS-COUNSEL`, per R-13 and the website
standing rules). Drafting now is preparation, like the website.*

Anchors: D-022 (grassroots + employer growth) · D-029/D-030 ($100/month,
adults only) · D-031/D-033 (insurance never required; members keep their
coverage and clinicians) · D-016 (onboarding as capacity gate; "let me
refer you well") · D-017 (bounded availability) · R-13 (no claims that
outrun evidence) · `docs/website/design.md` rule 3 (credential accuracy)
· website sketches standing rules (copy filter, canonical phrases, one
CTA).

---

## 1. The test every packet must pass

The person receiving the folder should be able to answer, within one
minute, without the physician present:

> *Why did this doctor leave this with me, who might benefit, and what
> am I supposed to do with it?*

The architecture is built backwards from that minute:

1. **Cover letter** — answers *why me* and *what to do with this*.
2. **One-page practice overview** — answers *what is this and who is it
   for* (for the recipient's own judgment).
3. **Patient brochure(s)** — the thing they actually *do* with it: hand
   one to a person who needs it.
4. **Business cards** — the smallest pass-along unit.
5. **One audience insert** — answers the one question specific to their
   seat (referral mechanics, employer mechanics, affordability).

Five things visible on opening. Nothing else.

## 2. What was considered and deliberately cut

The candidate list was larger. Cuts, with reasons — per the working
agreement that one piece has one job and the website remains the deep
source of truth:

| Candidate item | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Separate "practice model explainer" | **Cut — merged** | This *is* the one-pager. A flyer + an overview + a model explainer are three names for one job. |
| Standalone pricing / "how $100/month works" sheet | **Cut — merged** | Price belongs on the one-pager and brochure in two sentences. The full coverage-interaction detail (Medicare private contract, Medicaid acknowledgment, QMB exclusion) stays **website-only**: it is counsel-sensitive, easy to get subtly wrong in print, and will drift. Print states the price and links to the pricing page. |
| Telemedicine / home-visit mechanics sheet | **Cut — merged** | A "how visits work" panel of the brochure; depth lives on the How-it-works page. |
| **CV / credentials document** | **Cut from default packet** | Three reasons. (a) The full CV and research background already live at `/cv` — every print piece can point there. (b) The credential guardrails (internship at GW; no residency or board certification; IBLM in progress) mean a bare CV in a folder invites the residency question with nobody present to frame it honestly; the accurate one-sentence credential line, *in context* on the clinician insert, serves trust better than a document that raises questions it can't answer. (c) It reads as self-promotion in exactly the packets meant to feel modest. **Posture: printed CV available on request; clinician-facing inserts carry the exact credential sentence + the `/cv` link.** Owner to confirm. |
| Research background document | **Cut** | One line on the clinician insert + `/cv` link. |
| "QR codes" as an item | **Cut — it's a rule** | Not an item; a placement standard (§6): every piece carries the same two destinations. |
| Fax-back referral form for clinicians | **Cut for v1** | Creates PHI-handling obligations before systems exist, and DPC referral reality is simpler: the patient books the free conversation, or the clinician introduces by email with the patient's permission. The clinician insert states this plainly. Revisit post-launch if referrers ask for it. |
| Swag, stands, multiple brochure titles, testimonial sheets | **Cut** | Modesty, R-13 (no testimonials/outcomes pre-outcomes), and the five-excellent-pieces principle. A front-desk brochure stand is a *later, by-invitation* option, not packet contents. |

## 3. The core packet (every audience)

### 3.1 Cover letter

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | One-page letter on plain letterhead, addressed to the organization or named person. Printed, hand-signed. |
| Recipient | The decision-maker / gatekeeper who opens the packet. |
| Job | Answer *why this is on your desk* in the first two sentences; name who among the people they serve might benefit; say exactly what to do ("keep the overview, hand the brochures to anyone who fits, one email and I'll bring more"). It is also the only personalized piece. |
| Core or specific | **Core in function; audience-variant in content.** One template, four short variants sharing ~70% of their text with a personalized opening line per recipient. |
| Format / length | 1 page, ≤250 words, generous white space. |
| Copies per packet | 1. |
| Duplication | Exists nowhere else; the letter is the piece that cannot be replaced by the website. |

### 3.2 Practice overview one-pager

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | "DuBose, M.D. — at a glance." One sheet, front side: what the practice is, who it serves, what's included, the price, the honest limits; back side: short bio with the accurate credential line, photo, contact, QR block. |
| Recipient | The professional/gatekeeper — **not** patient-voiced. Written so they can judge relevance for the people they serve. |
| Job | The 60-second understanding. Also the piece that survives on file after brochures are given away. |
| Core or specific | **Core.** Identical in every packet. |
| Format / length | 1 sheet, duplex, standard letter. Structured for scanning: five labeled blocks, not paragraphs. |
| Copies per packet | 1 (2 for organizations with multiple decision-makers, noted in the letter). |
| Duplication | Condenses the homepage + pricing page. Acceptable: it is the offline proxy for the site. Reuses canonical phrases verbatim ("when my panel is full, it is full" · "that is the whole catch"). |

### 3.3 Patient brochure

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | One tri-fold, patient-voiced brochure. Panels: the situation it serves (the untangling story, per the messaging pillars) · what membership includes · how visits work (video / home when clinically appropriate / clinic space when needed) · $100/month and what it doesn't cover, in two honest sentences · the limits ("business hours; not an emergency service") · the free conversation + QR/URL. |
| Recipient | The person the organization hands it to — the prospective patient. The org is the distributor, not the audience. |
| Job | The *only* piece designed to leave the building. Everything else supports the moment someone says "you should talk to this doctor" and hands this over. |
| Core or specific | **Core.** One brochure for all audiences. Resist audience-variant brochures: inventory pain, drift risk, and the patient-facing story is the same story. |
| Format / length | Tri-fold, 6 panels, letter-size folded. |
| Copies per packet | 5–8, refillable on request (the letter says so). |
| Duplication | Condenses homepage + pricing + how-it-works. By design — most recipients of the brochure will still visit the site; the brochure's job is to get them there or to the free conversation. |

### 3.4 Business cards

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | Standard card: name, "physician — adult primary care," phone, email, site URL, small QR to the booking page. Credential-accurate title only. |
| Recipient | Anyone; passed person-to-person. |
| Job | Smallest unit of referral; survives wallets. |
| Core or specific | **Core.** |
| Format / length | Standard 3.5×2, uncoated stock matching the letterhead. |
| Copies per packet | 3–5 (2 in the folder's card slits, the rest clipped to the brochures). |
| Duplication | None meaningful. |

### 3.5 The audience insert (one per packet)

One additional sheet chosen by where the packet is going — §4. Never
more than one insert per packet.

## 4. Audience-specific inserts

Four inserts cover every listed audience. Rule: an audience earns its
own insert only when it has a **distinct question** the core can't
answer; otherwise it maps to an existing insert.

| Audience from the brief | Insert |
|---|---|
| Medical specialists, other physicians | **A — Clinicians** |
| Therapists, counselors, psychologists | **B — Mental-health professionals** |
| Employers, professional offices | **C — Employers** |
| Churches, community orgs, senior-serving orgs, gyms/wellness | **D — Community** |

### Insert A — Referring clinicians ("Working alongside your care")

- **Distinct question answered:** *does this compete with or complicate
  my care of the patient?*
- Content: who to consider referring (adults who lack a primary-care
  home, need more time than a visit allows, or need longitudinal
  coordination) · **complement, not competition** — patients keep their
  specialists, their physicians, and their insurance (D-033); the
  membership covers only this physician's work · how coordination and
  records-sharing work · how a patient starts (books the free
  conversation; or clinician introduction with the patient's
  permission) · the exact credential paragraph + `/cv` link · direct
  contact line.
- **Guardrail:** this is the most credential-sensitive audience.
  The insert states training plainly per design.md rule 3 — never
  implied residency or board certification. Counsel pass mandatory.
- Format: 1 sheet, single-sided. 1 copy per packet.
- Duplication: none — this content deliberately does not exist on the
  patient-facing website. The insert is its only home (until/unless a
  "For clinicians" page is added; if one is added, insert and page must
  share copy verbatim).

### Insert B — Mental-health professionals

A variant of A, ~60% shared copy, because therapists' distinct question
is different: *can this physician handle the medical side of my
clients' care — including medication — and collaborate with me?*

- Content: scope relevant to their clients (depression/anxiety
  medication management, ADHD, insomnia, sleep, weight, the
  stress–health tangle — all confirmed in-scope 2026-08-17) ·
  collaboration posture (therapy stays theirs; medical care and
  medication management coordinated with, not around, them) · how a
  client starts · credential paragraph.
- **Dependency:** ADHD/controlled-substance telemedicine policy is
  launch-critical (working model §5). This insert is drafted last and
  reviewed against that policy before printing.
- Rationale for a separate insert: given confirmed scope, therapists
  are plausibly the highest-fit referral audience in the whole list —
  they hold long-term relationships with exactly the overwhelmed adults
  the practice is designed for, and they chronically need a
  collaborating prescriber with time.
- Format: 1 sheet, single-sided. 1 copy.

### Insert C — Employers and professional offices

- **Distinct question answered:** *what does this cost me, and what am
  I allowed to do with it?*
- Content, two lanes in this order:
  1. **Free lane (the lead):** this is a resource you can make
     available to employees at no cost and no administration —
     employees who join pay for their own membership; the employer is
     handing over a brochure, not buying a benefit.
  2. **Sponsored lane (one short paragraph):** employer-sponsored
     memberships exist for employers who want to pay for some or all of
     the fee (D-022 — a researched, major DPC channel); "if that
     interests you, it's a conversation, not a brochure."
- What membership gives an employee, stated as *access facts only*:
  a physician with time, direct messaging, telemedicine convenience,
  prevention and chronic-condition care, help navigating the system.
- **Guardrail (R-13, explicit in the brief):** no productivity,
  absenteeism, healthcare-savings, or outcome claims of any kind. The
  researched market fact (many DPC members arrive via employers) may
  appear only if cited and counsel-passed; default is to omit numbers
  entirely. `REFRESH-AT-RUNWAY` on any market figure.
- Format: 1 sheet, single-sided. 1 copy.

### Insert D — Community, faith, and senior-serving organizations

- **Distinct question answered:** *can the people I serve actually
  afford and reach this — and is it safe to recommend?*
- Content, plain community register (website copy filter applies):
  a doctor with time to listen · $100/month, everything about my care
  included, cancel anytime · you keep your insurance and your other
  doctors · **for seniors: you keep Medicare** — one reassurance
  sentence, details deferred to the pricing page and the free
  conversation (the private-contract mechanics stay off print) · **the
  access rate, in the website's exact framing:** a limited number of
  reduced-rate memberships when the standard price would genuinely
  prevent care — no application, just a conversation · the free
  conversation as the zero-risk first step.
- Explicitly *not* sales-voiced: no urgency, no scarcity framing beyond
  the honest panel limit.
- Serves churches, community organizations, senior centers, and
  gyms/wellness groups. If gyms later prove a real channel with a
  distinct question (lifestyle-medicine emphasis), split then — not
  speculatively.
- Format: 1 sheet, single-sided. 1 copy.

**Total distinct designs to produce: 8** — letter template (with 4
variants), one-pager, brochure, card, inserts A–D. A recipient ever
sees **5 pieces**.

## 5. Physical presentation

- **Carrier:** one 9×12 two-pocket folder, heavy uncoated stock, name
  + wordmark only — stationery register, not sales-kit register. **Same
  folder for every audience** (modesty, inventory simplicity; the
  letter and insert do the tailoring). No glossy medical-practice
  folder; it should feel like it came from a person.
- **Reading order engineered:** right pocket, top to bottom = cover
  letter (loose, on top — first thing seen), one-pager, insert. Left
  pocket = brochures ×5–8; two cards in the folder's die-cut slits, the
  rest clipped to the brochure stack (a visual cue that these are for
  giving away).
- **Drop-off vs. mail:** drop-off gets the full folder. Mail gets a
  flat 9×12 envelope with letter + one-pager + insert + 2 brochures +
  1 card — no folder (cost and postage honesty; the letter offers to
  bring the full set in person, which creates the visit).
- **Personalization:** the letter only — recipient's name, one sentence
  about *their* organization, hand signature. Nothing else is
  personalized, ever; that keeps personalization sustainable at
  grassroots volume.
- **QR/link standard:** every piece carries at most the same two
  destinations — the site, and the booking page ("Book a free
  conversation," the site-wide single CTA) — always with the
  human-readable URL printed beside the code. Placement: brochure back
  panel, one-pager footer, insert CTA block, card back.
- **Refill mechanism:** the letter's closing line — brochures replaced
  with one email, delivered in person. Cheap, personal, and creates the
  second visit, which is the actual relationship-building event
  (D-022: growth is grassroots relationships, and the packet is the
  artifact of the relationship, not a substitute for it).

## 6. Duplication rules (what print must never fork)

The website is the deep source; print summarizes and routes. Never
reproduced in print: the coverage-interaction table (Medicare/
Medicaid/QMB mechanics), the membership agreement, the FAQ, the CV, the
access-rate criteria. Print states each in ≤2 sentences and points to
the page. Canonical phrases are reused verbatim wherever they appear.
Any figure that appears in print gets `REFRESH-AT-RUNWAY`.

## 7. Standing guardrails (bind every piece)

1. R-13: process, time, attention, personalization — never outcomes,
   savings, productivity, or testimonials that don't exist yet.
2. Credential accuracy (design.md rule 3) on every piece that mentions
   the physician; the clinician inserts carry the exact sentence.
3. Bounded availability stated, not hidden: business hours, not an
   emergency service, real time away (D-017). No concierge or 24/7
   implication anywhere.
4. $100/month, adults only, cancel anytime (D-029/D-030/D-027) —
   identical everywhere. No legacy $5,000/year or luxury-concierge
   framing (archived; stays archived).
5. No PHI mechanics in v1: referral = patient books, or clinician
   introduces with permission.
6. Website standing rules apply to print: no emojis, copy filter,
   one CTA, no dark patterns.
7. Everything gets the TMB advertising/counsel pass before printing
   (`NEEDS-COUNSEL`); [City], licensure, and market figures are
   placeholders until runway.
