# Book a Conversation (+ states)

**Job:** don't lose the decided visitor; set the conversation up well.
Near-zero friction, expectations in one glance, the emergency taxonomy
one final time, confirmation states that feel like a person.
**Architectural rule: nothing clinical is collected — name, email,
phone, referral source only. The scheduler stays outside PHI.**

## State A — The booking page (desktop: what-to-expect left ~40%, scheduler right ~60%)

> # Book a free conversation

**Left — What to expect:**

> Thirty minutes, by video. You tell me what's going on — or just that
> you're curious. I'll ask some questions, answer yours, and tell you
> honestly whether I can help.
>
> A few things you should know going in:
> - It's free, and there is no obligation to join.
> - If I'm not the right fit, I'll say so and point you toward someone
>   good.
> - No preparation needed. Don't gather records or rehearse anything —
>   just come as you are.
> - This conversation is not medical care, and nothing about it commits
>   you (or me) to anything.

**Right — embedded scheduler:** Step 1: pick a time (business-hours
slots, 30 min; defined blocks per the slot policy below). Step 2: three
fields — Name, Email, Phone — plus optional "How did you hear about
me?" dropdown (friend or family / my doctor / online / other).
[ Confirm booking ]. Under the button:

> No medical details are needed to book — save those for our
> conversation.

**Below both columns, full width, quiet bordered box:**

> **Before you book — the one thing that never changes:** If you are
> having an emergency — chest pain, stroke signs, severe trouble
> breathing, thoughts of self-harm — call 911 or go to the nearest ER
> now. This page cannot help you today; the emergency room can.

## Mobile

Columns stack: what-to-expect first, scheduler second (reassurance
precedes the calendar); emergency box between them; scheduler vendor's
mobile UI quality is a selection criterion — most word-of-mouth
visitors arrive on phones.

## Decisions of record

Three fields and a deliberate refusal to collect more (the absence made
visible) · "no preparation needed — come as you are" · confirmation
states in the physician's voice from his real address (the
anti-automation aesthetic; also the Track F demo kit) · "change or
cancel — no explanation needed" in every state · the waitlist converts
scarcity into integrity and refers people elsewhere · no sticky bar, no
competing CTAs on this page.

## Open (operating decisions, not sketch blockers)

Slot policy: defined consultation blocks (recommended) vs. open
calendar — lives with the physician's calendar. Phone field required
(drafted) vs. optional — soften with "only used for this conversation"
microcopy if desired. State D referral links need real local
alternatives once the city is real.
