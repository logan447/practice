## Group 1 — The model

**Is this health insurance?** No. It is a direct agreement between you
and me for my services as your physician. It does not replace
insurance, and I recommend every member keep coverage for everything
outside our relationship — labs, medications, specialists, hospitals.

**Is this "concierge medicine"?** It shares one idea with concierge
practices — a small panel and real access — without the rest. Concierge
fees typically run $2,400 to $5,000 a year on top of insurance billing.
This is $100 a month, I bill no one, and there are no tiers. The formal
name for the model is direct primary care.

**Why is it only $100 a month? What's the catch?** The price works
because the practice is small by design: no office building, no billing
department, no staff, and a limited number of patients. The limits are
stated rather than hidden: I keep business hours, I am not an emergency
service, I take real time away each year, and when my panel is full it
is full. That is the whole catch.

**I already have doctors I like. Does this still make sense?** Often,
yes. You can keep every doctor you have — including another primary
care physician. Members in that situation use me as the doctor who has
time: to explain, to coordinate, to watch the whole picture between
everyone else's appointments.

**What does "unhurried" actually mean?** Visits are scheduled for as
long as they need — a first visit is up to 90 minutes, and no visit
ends because a schedule says so. I keep my panel small enough that this
stays true.

## Group 2 — Money & coverage

**Besides $100 a month, what will I pay?** Nothing to me. Care
delivered by others is billed by them, usually through your insurance:
labs, imaging, medications, vaccines, specialists, urgent care,
hospitals. Where I can get discounted direct lab pricing, I pass it
through at my cost and tell you the number before anything is drawn.
More: Pricing →

**Are there extra fees for anything? Forms? Missed visits?** No.
Paperwork — disability and FMLA forms, letters, prior authorizations —
is part of the job, not an upsell. There are no no-show fees; if
scheduling stops working we will talk about it. The only pass-throughs
are procedure supplies at my cost, named before the procedure.

**I'm on Medicare. Can I join?** Yes. Federal rules require one extra
document — a Medicare private contract — because I have formally opted
out of billing Medicare for my services. Your Medicare continues to pay
for labs, imaging, specialists, prescriptions, and hospital care
exactly as before. More: Pricing →

**I'm on Medicaid. Can I join?** Yes, with one signed acknowledgment
required by state rules. If you have traditional fee-for-service
Medicaid we will talk first about how tests and prescriptions I order
are covered. One exception: federal rules bar private arrangements for
certain combined Medicare-Medicaid programs (QMB) — if that is your
situation, I will help you find good care instead.

**Can I pay with my HSA?** Possibly — recent federal law may allow HSA
funds for direct primary care fees like this one. Confirm with your tax
advisor; I will give you the documentation you need.

**What if $100 a month is genuinely out of reach?** Tell me. I keep a
limited number of reduced-rate memberships for people for whom the
standard price would prevent care. It is a conversation, not an
application.

## Group 3 — Availability

**How quickly do you respond to messages?** Within one to two business
days, usually faster during business hours. If something needs
attention sooner, say so in the message. If it is urgent or an
emergency, see the next answer — messaging is never the path for those.

**What about nights, weekends, and emergencies?** I keep business
hours, Monday through Friday, and I do not promise 24/7 access.
Emergencies — chest pain, stroke signs, severe injury, thoughts of
self-harm — are always 911 and the ER. Urgent same-day problems when I
am unavailable: urgent care, then tell me and I will fold what happened
into your care. This is what keeps the attention real for everyone the
rest of the time.

**What happens when you are away?** I take genuine time away each year.
You will know in advance, I still check incoming results and messages
periodically for anything important, refills are squared away before I
go, and the emergency and urgent-care pathways work exactly as always.
Away means slower — never unaware.

**Do you make house calls?** Yes, when an examination genuinely needs
it and you are within my service area — scheduled, at no extra charge.
Home visits are a clinical tool, not an on-demand service; most care
happens by video, with clinic space when a procedure or exam needs a
proper room.

## Group 4 — The medicine

**What do you treat?** Full-scope adult primary care: blood pressure,
diabetes, cholesterol, thyroid, everyday illness, prevention and
screening, depression and anxiety, ADHD, weight management including
modern medications, sleep problems, and the tangled situations where
health and life affect each other. More: What I can help with →

**What is your training, exactly?** M.D. from the Texas A&M College of
Medicine; internal medicine internship at George Washington University;
years of part-time primary care practice alongside leading NIH-funded
research on aging and access to care; currently pursuing lifestyle
medicine certification. I did not complete a residency, and I will
never imply otherwise — precision about my qualifications is where
trust starts. The full picture, stated plainly: About →

**Do you prescribe controlled medications?** Within a careful written
policy, yes — including ADHD medications where appropriate. Federal and
Texas telemedicine rules apply, some medications require in-person
evaluation or monitoring, and I prescribe conservatively. If your
primary goal is a specific controlled prescription, I am probably not
the right fit — if your goal is to be well, we will use every tool that
makes sense.

**What if I need a specialist or end up in the hospital?** Then you
will have the right specialist, with a real referral and a doctor who
stays involved — I coordinate, translate, and keep the whole picture.
In the hospital, the hospital's physicians lead; I stay informed and
manage your landing when you are home.

**Can you order labs and imaging?** Yes — ordered when needed,
explained always. Labs run through your insurance or, where cheaper, at
discounted direct prices I pass through at cost, told to you in
advance.

## Group 5 — Starting & stopping

**What is the free conversation like?** Thirty minutes by video. You
tell me what is going on; I ask questions; I tell you honestly whether
I can help. No forms beyond your name and email, no pressure, and no
bill. If I am not the right fit, I will say so and point you toward
someone good.

**How do I join, and how fast can we start?** After we talk: sign the
membership agreement, add a payment method, and we schedule your first
full visit — usually within the first two weeks, after I have read your
records.

**Is there a contract or minimum commitment?** No. Month to month from
day one, no enrollment fee, cancel anytime effective at the end of your
billing month.

**What happens to my records if I leave?** They are yours. A complete
electronic copy is always free, sent promptly to you and your next
physician, active issues are safely handed off, and prescriptions are
bridged up to 90 days where clinically safe.

**Can my spouse or family join?** Any adult can join — each with their
own membership at the same $100. A couple is $200 a month. I care for
adults only, so children need a pediatrician — I happen to be married
to one, so I say that with respect.
