# Pricing & Insurance

**Job:** the skeptic's page. Answers in descending order of anxiety,
hides nothing, lets the numbers persuade. Includes the approved
affordability architecture (Option B access rate, X-10) and the
paperwork-included amendment.

## Section order and copy

### 1 · Intro

> # Pricing
> One number, explained completely. If anything here is unclear, ask me
> directly — that is what the free conversation is for.

### 2 · The price card (light band; single centered card, the page's anchor)

> ## $100 per month
> per adult patient
>
> **Included, with no per-visit charges:**
> - Visits as your care needs them — video, home when clinically
>   appropriate, clinic space when needed
> - Direct messaging with me, answered in 1–2 business days
> - Every result reviewed and explained
> - Prescriptions managed; refills handled without games
> - Referrals and coordination across your other doctors
> - Forms and paperwork handled — disability, FMLA, insurance forms,
>   letters, prior authorizations
> - An unhurried annual review of everything
>
> No enrollment fee. No contract. No commitment. Cancel anytime,
> effective at the end of your billing month. Monthly by card or bank
> account; annual prepayment available at the same price.
>
> [ Book a free conversation ]

> Each adult enrolls individually: a couple is $200, three adult family
> members $300. I care for adults only.

### 3 · If $100 is a real barrier (white; the access rate, Option B / X-10)

> ## If $100 a month is a real barrier
> I keep a limited number of reduced-rate memberships for people for
> whom the standard price would genuinely prevent care. There is no
> application form and no means test — it is a conversation, and I keep
> simple written criteria so it is fair. If cost is the thing stopping
> you, say so when we talk.

### 4 · What the fee does not cover — and how those get paid (light band; item / how-paid rows)

> My fee covers my work as your physician. Care delivered by others is
> billed by them, usually through your insurance:

- **Labs** — Through your insurance — or, where I can arrange
  discounted direct pricing, passed to you at my cost, told to you
  before anything is drawn. Often a fraction of list price.
- **Imaging** — Through your insurance; I order what is needed and
  explain the results.
- **Medications** — Your pharmacy and insurance as now. I help you find
  fair prices; Texas law does not allow physicians to sell medications.
- **Specialists** — Through your insurance; I refer carefully and stay
  involved.
- **Urgent care, ER, hospital** — Through your insurance, as today. My
  fee never replaces coverage for these.

> This is why I recommend every member keep health coverage: the
> membership works alongside insurance, not instead of it.

### 5 · How your coverage fits (white; "one extra step" rows)

> Whatever insurance you have — or none — you can join. The price and
> the care are the same for everyone. Government programs add one
> signature:

- **Private insurance or none** — Sign up. That is all.
- **Medicare** — One additional document — a Medicare private
  contract — is required by federal rules. Your Medicare continues to
  pay for labs, imaging, specialists, prescriptions, and hospital care
  exactly as before. My fee covers only my services.
- **Medicare Advantage** — Same private contract. If your plan is an
  HMO, we will talk through how your plan handles tests I order before
  you join.
- **Texas Medicaid** — A short private-pay acknowledgment is required
  by state rules. Your Medicaid continues to work outside our
  relationship; if you have traditional (non-plan) Medicaid, we will
  discuss how orders are covered first.

> The one exception: federal rules do not permit private arrangements
> with patients in certain combined Medicare and Medicaid programs (QMB
> and similar). If that is your situation, I will help you find good
> care instead.
>
> Using HSA funds: recent federal law may allow it for memberships like
> this one. Confirm with your tax advisor.

### 6 · Why $100, honestly (light band; researched anchors, REFRESH-AT-RUNWAY)

> Concierge medicine typically charges $2,400 to $5,000 a year in
> membership fees — and usually still bills your insurance for visits.
> A single cash-pay office visit commonly runs $130 to $200. Direct
> primary care practices like this one average about $100 a month
> nationally.
>
> I can charge this because the practice is small by design: no office
> building, no billing department, no volume targets — a limited number
> of patients, each of whom I actually know. The price is not
> subsidized by anything. It is what unhurried primary care costs when
> the overhead is honest.

### 7 · What's the catch? (white; amended — paperwork removed from fee list)

> There are limits, and they are stated rather than hidden: I keep
> business hours and am not an emergency service. I take real time away
> each year. And when my panel is full, it is full; I would rather
> close enrollment than dilute attention. That is the whole catch.
>
> Read the full membership agreement (PDF) — yes, before you ever talk
> to me.
