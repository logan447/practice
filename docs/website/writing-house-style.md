# Writing House Style — DuBose, M.D.

*Source of truth for how the practice writes, distilled from the
approved copy iterations (2026-08). Companion to
`design-house-style.md`. Applies to the website, patient materials,
outreach, and future articles.*

## The standard

Clear, thoughtful, direct, concise, mature, and simple. Roughly a
5th-grade reading level. Plain and slightly dry beats clever. The
reader should think "I understand exactly what this doctor does," not
"this is well-written marketing."

## Rules

1. Short sentences. Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences).
2. Common words: "medicines" not "medications" in patient-facing copy,
   "doctor" alongside "physician," no jargon without explanation.
3. **No em dashes.** Use periods, commas, or colons. Middle dots for
   title separators.
4. No emojis, anywhere, ever.
5. No marketing language, urgency, superlatives, or unnecessary
   adjectives. Concrete statements over emotional framing.
6. No clever taglines or cute phrasing where plain language works.
7. Headings say exactly what the section is about.
8. Once a point is made, stop. No restatements or closing subtext.
9. Terminology is consistent across pages: "membership,"
   "the free conversation," "Book a free conversation" (the one CTA),
   "membership agreement," "patient login."
10. Second person for the patient, first person for the physician.
    The physician speaks as "I"; the practice does not speak as "we"
    except for physician-and-patient together ("we work on it").

## Referral safety (binding)

Materials may be handed to a patient by their own physician. Nothing
may state or imply that a patient's current doctors lack time,
don't listen, or provide inadequate care. Describe what this practice
does; never contrast it with anyone else's care. Banned patterns:
"a doctor with time" as an implied comparison, "nobody has time,"
"results nobody explained," "not just refilled," "more than 15
minutes," "no one holding the whole picture." The practice is an
added layer of support, complementary to a patient's existing
clinicians.

## Claims discipline (binding)

- No outcome promises. Describe goals and process; "we work on them
  and measure," never "you will improve."
- Credentials exactly true: internal medicine **internship** at George
  Washington University — never state or imply completed residency or
  board certification in internal/family medicine. Lifestyle medicine
  certification is **expected January 2027** — say "expected," never
  earned, until it is. Self-description: "physician" / "primary care
  doctor," never "internist."
- No invented numbers, policies, or credentials. Market figures carry
  a source and a refresh date in the repo.
- Safety content is never trimmed for style: emergency pathways (911 /
  ER, urgent care, routine messaging) keep their full form wherever
  they appear.
- All public copy passes the TMB-advertising/counsel review before
  launch (R-13).

## Voice touchstones

Honesty is the brand. State limits plainly ("when my panel is full, it
is full"; "I keep business hours"). Never position against other
clinicians — the practice is complementary, not a rescue. Respect the
reader's time: every sentence earns its place or goes.
