# Medicare & Medicaid Members — The Q-21 Brief

*Researched 2026-08-17 from primary sources (42 CFR, SSA, CMS/MLN, 1 TAC,
TMPPM/TMHP, Texas VDP). `REFRESH-AT-RUNWAY`. This brief answers: can an
adult keep Medicare or Medicaid and voluntarily pay $100/month for this
physician relationship — and under what structure? Findings feed the
Patient Agreement's Eligibility provision and the counsel agenda. Nothing
here is legal advice; the closing section lists what counsel must
confirm.*

**The design principle being tested (user-stated):** insurance is a
financing mechanism for healthcare elsewhere in the patient's life; it
should not determine whether a physician-patient relationship can exist.

## Part A — Texas Medicaid (researched; primary sources verified)

### The headline: conditionally YES

A Texas physician who is **not enrolled** in Medicaid may accept a
Medicaid-enrolled adult as a **voluntary private-pay patient**:

- 42 CFR 447.15 ("payment in full") binds *participating* providers
  through the provider agreement; it does not reach a physician with no
  Medicaid enrollment.
- 1 TAC §354.1131(f) expressly contemplates it: a non-participating
  provider *should inform* the recipient before service that no Medicaid
  claim will be filed; the recipient is then directly responsible for
  payment.
- TMPPM §1.7.12.1 makes the writing functionally mandatory: without
  **signed written notification** of private-pay status, the provider
  *cannot collect from the patient at all*. TMHP publishes a model form
  (**F00072 Private Pay Agreement**) — notably *period-based*, which fits
  a membership term naturally.

### The two hard limits

1. **Dual eligibles (Medicare + Medicaid, incl. QMB/MQMB) are
   effectively off-limits.** TMHP (2015 bulletin): providers "cannot
   accept QMB or MQMB clients as 'private pay' to bill the client
   directly," *even if not enrolled in Medicaid*; federal law (SSA
   §1902(n)(3)(B), §1866(a)(1)(A); CMS MLN SE1128) prohibits billing
   QMBs, and CMS/KFF guidance indicates even Medicare **opt-out private
   contracts** are prohibited with full duals/QMBs. → The membership must
   screen for and exclude dual/QMB status. `ESTABLISHED/DIRECTIONAL`
2. **The downstream (ORP) problem.** Texas requires the
   ordering/referring/prescribing physician to be Medicaid-enrolled (at
   least ORP-only) for **fee-for-service** Medicaid to pay downstream
   claims — labs, imaging, DME, and **pharmacy** claims ordered by a
   non-enrolled physician **will be denied** for FFS members (TMPPM
   §1.1.4.2, §6.4.2.4; VDP Manual §5.4.6). **Managed-care (STAR/
   STAR+PLUS) members are exempt from the state ORP edit** for
   out-of-network orderers — and most Texas adult Medicaid is managed
   care — but individual MCO/PBM edits and referral rules are unverified
   plan-by-plan. `ESTABLISHED` as to manuals; `COULD-NOT-VERIFY` MCO
   behavior.

### The structural options for downstream coverage

- **Option 1 — stay fully un-enrolled:** Medicaid members are accepted
  with the private-pay acknowledgment; for FFS members, orders and
  prescriptions route through an enrolled clinician (e.g., their
  MCO-assigned PCP) — workable but undercuts the value proposition; for
  MCO members, downstream likely works subject to plan-level rules.
- **Option 2 — ORP-only (non-billing) enrollment via PEMS:** fixes FFS
  downstream denials; the physician still never bills Medicaid. **The
  pivotal unresolved question (counsel):** whether ORP-only enrollment
  imports §354.1131(b)/(h) payment-in-full or nondiscrimination duties
  that would prohibit charging Medicaid members privately for his own
  services. If it does not (the manuals' structure suggests ORP-only is
  a screening status, not a billing relationship), this is the clean
  answer. `COULD-NOT-VERIFY — top Medicaid counsel question.`

### Enrolled-provider contrast (why we stay out)

An *enrolled* provider faces payment-in-full and nondiscrimination
duties, must offer any advertised discount to Medicaid clients, and can
accept private pay only in narrow, patient-initiated, episode-specific
circumstances — a systematic membership offering while enrolled as a
billing provider would be high-risk. Staying un-enrolled (or ORP-only,
pending counsel) is the compatible posture.

### Practical structure for Medicaid members (pending counsel)

1. Enrollment screening: verify coverage status; **exclude dual/QMB**;
   re-verify periodically (eligibility changes monthly).
2. **Before membership starts:** signed private-pay agreement modeled on
   F00072, expanded to state the membership term, that no claims will be
   filed with Medicaid or any MCO, and full financial responsibility;
   re-executed at each renewal; retained in the chart.
3. Written plain-language warning about the downstream gap (per Option
   1/2 resolution).
4. No marketing targeted at Medicaid enrollees until counsel clears the
   inducement/steering question (no Texas authority found; residual
   risk).
