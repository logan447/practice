## Agreement for Direct Primary Care Membership

**[Practice Name — LATER]** · [Physician name], M.D. · Texas
*This is a direct primary care medical service agreement under Texas
Occupations Code ch. 162, subch. F. It is not health insurance.* `[COUNSEL]`

### The short version (one page, part of the agreement)

- **You pay $100 per month.** That's the whole price for my services as
  your physician. No per-visit charges, no tiers, no packages.
- **You get a physician who knows you** and has time to help you think
  through your health — visits by video as often as your care genuinely
  needs, messaging between visits, your results explained, your
  medications managed, your care coordinated, and a plan we build
  together.
- **Where care happens:** mostly by telemedicine. I come to you when an
  examination genuinely needs it and it's practical; occasionally we'll
  meet at clinical space I use for exams and procedures.
- **When I'm available:** business hours, Monday–Friday. I typically
  respond to routine messages within 1–2 business days. I take real time
  away; I'll always tell you in advance, and I still monitor for
  important results while away.
- **I am not an emergency service.** Emergencies → 911/ER. Urgent
  same-day problems when I'm unavailable → urgent care. This never
  changes, and your membership never replaces it.
- **What's not in the price:** things other people bill for — labs,
  imaging, medications, vaccines, specialists, hospitals, urgent care.
  Your insurance keeps doing its job there. Also a short list of unusual
  services I'll always name before any charge (§7).
- **Leaving is easy:** cancel anytime, effective at the end of your
  current billing month. I'll make sure the ending is medically safe.

### 1. Our relationship

1.1. By signing, you and I establish a physician–patient relationship.
You are enrolling as an individual adult (18+). Each adult in a household
enrolls separately under their own agreement. I do not provide pediatric
care.

1.2. This is a **direct-pay** relationship: I do not bill your health
insurance, Medicare, or Medicaid for my services, and your monthly fee is
not insurance and does not satisfy any insurance-coverage requirement.
You are encouraged to maintain health coverage for everything outside
this agreement (§6). *[Statutory notice wording per Tex. Occ. Code
§162.254 — `COUNSEL`]*

1.3. **You may keep or use other physicians**, including another primary
care physician, at any time. If you do, I'll coordinate with them when
you want me to.

1.4. **Eligibility screening.** I currently cannot enroll patients who
are enrolled in Medicare or Medicaid; enrollment includes confirming your
coverage status. *[This is the current posture pending counsel on Q-21 —
Medicare rules restrict what fees may be charged to enrolled
beneficiaries. Revisit language once counsel resolves what can be
offered. `COUNSEL` — top priority]*

### 2. What your membership includes

2.1. **My time and attention as your physician**, individualized to what
your health actually needs — not a predetermined package. Depending on
your situation this may include: prevention and lifestyle medicine;
acute-problem care; chronic disease management; medication prescribing
and management; ordering and interpreting labs, imaging, and other
diagnostics; mental well-being within my scope; attention to the life
context that affects health (stress, work, relationships,
financial-stress factors) within my scope; referral to and coordination
with specialists; and longitudinal follow-through.

2.2. **Visits, without per-visit charges.** Video visits as clinically
appropriate — some months none, some months several. There is no visit
quota and no per-visit fee. *(Frequency is guided by clinical need and my
professional judgment; membership is access to appropriate care, not a
standing entitlement to a fixed schedule.)*

2.3. **Between-visit communication** through the secure portal: your
questions, my interpretation of every result, refill management, and
plan adjustments. Routine responses within **1–2 business days**;
messages needing action sooner should say so, and anything urgent
belongs in §5's pathways.

2.4. **In-person care** in two forms:
- **Home visits**, when in my clinical judgment an examination or
  service genuinely requires being with you and a home visit is
  practical. Home visits are scheduled (typically clustered by
  geography) and are included in your membership within my service area
  of **[radius/area — LATER, set with the launch city]**.
  *[`DECISION` — recommendation: no separately-charged "convenience"
  home-visit tier at launch; home visits are a clinical tool at my
  judgment, not an on-demand amenity. Revisit only if demand data says
  otherwise.]*
- **Clinic visits** at rented clinical space, for examinations and
  procedures better done in an equipped room — included in your
  membership.

2.5. **An annual (or otherwise agreed) whole-picture review** — an
unhurried look at everything: trends, medications, screening, goals.

2.6. **Your health, visible.** Your portal shows your trends — the
measures we agree matter for you (blood pressure, labs, weight, symptoms,
function, well-being) — so both of us can see whether care is working.

### 3. What I commit to

- To know you — your history, context, and goals — and to bring real
  time and thought to your care.
- Evidence-based medicine: I'll tell you what the evidence supports,
  what it doesn't, and what I don't know.
- Honesty about scope: if your situation needs more than I can provide
  well (§8), I'll say so and help you get to the right care.
- Clear expectations: response times, availability, time away, and costs
  as written here — no surprises.

### 4. What I do not promise

- **24/7 availability, or evening/weekend availability.** I am one
  physician with a deliberately sustainable practice; that is what makes
  the rest of this possible.
- Emergency or urgent-response capability (§5).
- Specific clinical outcomes. Good medicine improves the odds; it cannot
  guarantee results, and I won't pretend otherwise.
- Hospital care. If you're hospitalized, the hospital's physicians manage
  the admission; I stay informed, share records as appropriate, and
  coordinate your return to normal care.
- That every request fits the membership (§7).

### 5. Emergencies, urgent needs, and my time away

5.1. **Emergency (call 911 / go to the ER):** chest pain, stroke
symptoms, severe breathing difficulty, major injury, uncontrolled
bleeding, thoughts of self-harm — anything that could threaten life,
limb, or organ. **Never wait for me.**

5.2. **Urgent (same-day, can't wait for my response):** use an urgent
care center or your other physicians. Tell me afterward; I'll integrate
what happened into your care.

5.3. **Routine:** message me — 1–2 business-day response, usually faster
during business hours.

5.4. **My time away.** I take substantial planned time away each year —
up to roughly three months in total across the year `[CARRIER — the Q-17
verification covers whether this structure needs more formal coverage]`.
When I'm away: I tell you in advance through the portal; I continue to
monitor incoming results and messages periodically for anything
important; refills and stable care are arranged before I go; and §5.1/5.2
pathways handle what can't wait. "Away" means slower and less available —
never unaware of something critical in an active workup.

5.5. **If I'm ever suddenly unable to practice** (illness, emergency),
the practice will notify you promptly, your records remain available to
you immediately, and billing pauses until regular care resumes.
`[COUNSEL — contingency and wind-down provisions; carrier expectations]`

### 6. What your membership does NOT pay for (third-party services)

Your fee covers **my** services only. Billed by others, at their prices,
typically through your insurance:

- laboratory testing (where I can arrange discounted cash pricing, I'll
  pass it through at my cost, disclosed in advance — often dramatically
  cheaper than list price)
- imaging and other diagnostics
- medications and vaccines (Texas law does not permit me to dispense;
  I'll help you find fair prices)
- specialists, therapists, and other clinicians
- urgent care, ER, hospital, and ambulance services
- medical equipment and supplies you take home

**Your insurance still matters.** Keeping coverage protects you for
everything above. Note for high-deductible/HSA plans: recent federal law
may allow HSA funds to pay direct primary care fees like this one —
confirm with your tax advisor before doing so. `[TAX/COUNSEL — verify the
practice's arrangement qualifies under the post-2025 rules before this
sentence ships]`

### 7. The short list of things outside the membership

I will always tell you before anything generates a charge. Outside the
monthly fee: `[DECISION — this section is the Q-19 carve-out list;
recommendations below, none yet policy]`

- **Extensive third-party paperwork:** disability/FMLA/insurance forms
  beyond simple ones, letters for legal matters, deposition or record
  review for litigation — *recommended: published flat or hourly fee*
- **Travel outside the service area** for home visits — *recommended:
  simply outside scope rather than surcharged; telemedicine or clinic
  instead*
- **Procedure-specific supplies** with real cost — *recommended: at-cost
  pass-through, stated before the procedure*
- **Care episodes requiring near-daily physician contact for an extended
  period** — *recommended: not a fee trigger; instead an honest
  conversation about whether this practice is still the right level of
  care (§8) — money is the wrong lever for intensity*
- No-show/late-cancellation fees: *recommended: none — visits are not
  billed, and chronic no-shows are a fit conversation, not a fee*

### 8. Scope and its edges

My practice is broad-scope adult primary care with lifestyle medicine.
Some situations need more than a solo primary care practice should
provide alone — examples: conditions requiring specialist-level ongoing
management I'll co-manage rather than own; psychiatric care beyond
primary-care-appropriate management; substance-use treatment beyond its
safe primary-care point; any situation needing rapid in-person response
or continuous monitoring. In these cases my job is honest triage,
referral, and coordination — not pretending otherwise. If your needs
outgrow what I can deliver well, I'll tell you directly and help you
transition to the right care (§10 protections apply).

**Controlled substances:** prescribed only within a conservative written
policy (in-person/telemedicine rules, monitoring, agreements as
required). `[COUNSEL/RESEARCH — federal telemedicine controlled-substance
rules are in flux and Texas rules apply; policy drafted before launch]`

**Location:** telemedicine requires me to be licensed where you are
during the visit. My license covers Texas `[+ Virginia status — LATER]`.
If you travel or move, tell me — short trips are usually manageable
(within rules); relocation may end eligibility (§10).

### 10. Ending the relationship

- **You:** cancel anytime via the portal or in writing — effective at
  the end of your current paid month. No fees, no questions required
  (though I'll always welcome knowing why).
- **Me:** I may end the relationship with **30 days' written notice**
  `[COUNSEL — TMB non-abandonment norms]`, during which I remain
  available for urgent needs and active issues. I will do this rarely
  and never mid-crisis.
- **Either way, the ending is medically safe:** active workups completed
  or responsibly handed off with a written summary; prescriptions
  bridged (up to [90] days where clinically safe) while you establish
  new care; your complete records available to you and your next
  physician promptly `[COUNSEL — records fees/timing under Texas rules;
  recommendation: free electronic copy, always]`.
- If membership ends for any reason, charges stop with the membership;
  there is nothing to refund because months are paid as they're used.
  *(Annual-prepay patients: unused full months refunded pro-rata.)*

## What drafting this exposed (design findings)

**Contradictions with the foundation: none found.** The agreement wrote
cleanly from the current model — a good sign the re-baseline is coherent.
Notable confirmations: the panel-mix discipline (R-26) correctly lives
*outside* the agreement (in enrollment and renewal conversations), so the
patient-facing document contains no quotas; §2.2's "clinical
appropriateness" language is the only place utilization management
touches the patient, and it reads as medicine, not rationing.

**Decisions the draft forces (each has a recommendation inline above):**

1. **Medicare/Medicaid enrollment posture at launch** (§1.4) — drafted as
   "cannot enroll," the conservative reading, pending Q-21 counsel. This
   is the draft's most consequential placeholder.
2. **The §7 carve-out list** — this *is* Q-19 v1. Needs your yes/no per
   item, especially: paperwork fees, and the recommendation *against*
   no-show fees and *against* charging for high-intensity episodes.
3. **Card-on-file required?** (§9) — recommended yes; needs confirmation
   plus a counsel sanity check.
4. **Enrollment fee** (§9) — recommended $0 for founding cohort.
5. **Re-enrollment friction** (§9) — recommended conversation + capacity,
   no fee.
6. **Fee-change notice period** (§9) — 60 vs 90 days.
7. **Prescription bridge length at termination** (§10) — drafted [90]
   days where safe.
8. **Home-visit service area** (§2.4) — placeholder until the city is
   real.
9. **Sudden-incapacity contingency** (§5.5) — drafted thin; needs real
   design (who notifies patients, how records flow) — new operational
   question for the runway, and a carrier conversation.
10. **Controlled-substances policy** (§8) — needs its own research +
    counsel pass before launch; telemedicine prescribing rules are
    genuinely unsettled.

**Counsel package implications:** §§1.2, 1.4, 5.4–5.5, 8 (controlled
substances), 9 (GFE), 10 (non-abandonment, records), 11 — consistent
with the Q-15/Q-17/Q-21 briefs already planned; nothing new beyond
§5.5's contingency design.
