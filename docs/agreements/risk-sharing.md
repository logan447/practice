# Upfront Payment & Risk-Sharing — Pressure Test

*Analyzed 2026-08-16. Question: should patients pay upfront, and should the
practice offer some form of value guarantee? Goal feeling:*

> *"This doctor is confident enough in the value of his work that he is
> willing to make the economics fair if this relationship does not work
> for me."*

*Conclusion first: **achieve that feeling structurally — defined engagement
periods, automatic pro-rata refunds, and one narrow satisfaction provision
at the entry point — and do not offer an outcomes- or value-based
guarantee.** Reasoning below, so the decision can be re-argued later.*

---

## 1. The outcomes/value guarantee, pressure-tested honestly

**The appeal is real:** differentiation, demonstrated confidence, genuine
risk-sharing with the patient — psychologically aligned with everything
this practice is.

**Why it fails anyway:**

| Angle | Finding |
|---|---|
| Subjectivity | "Value received" has no objective trigger. The refund conversation at checkpoint is a *dispute about worth* with the exact person whose trust the practice runs on — the mechanism poisons what it was built to protect. |
| Moral hazard & selection | A money-back promise selectively attracts refund-optimizers and gives ambivalent patients a reason to under-engage (non-adherence *causes* the "no value" outcome that triggers the refund). |
| Work-already-done | The intensive archetypes (C, U) front-load physician work — records deep-dives, extended evaluations, workup design. A guarantee refunds the period when the most irreversible work happened. |
| Medical-legal | A refund can be characterized as an implicit admission that care was deficient; conditioning refunds on liability releases is ethically and legally fraught (`NEEDS-COUNSEL` — but flagged as a known trap, not a maybe). Guarantee marketing also collides with medical-board advertising norms: guaranteeing outcomes of medical care is a classic prohibited/restricted claim category — Texas specifics `NEEDS-COUNSEL`, but the design should not depend on winning that argument. |
| Regulatory | For any Medicare/Medicaid-adjacent patient, refund promises can implicate beneficiary-inducement rules (`payment-landscape.md` §1); a value-contingent fee also pushes a Texas DPC-statute agreement toward risk-bearing territory the safe harbor wasn't written for. Both `NEEDS-COUNSEL`; both avoidable by not building the feature. |
| Expectations | Medicine is probabilistic. An outcomes guarantee teaches the patient the opposite — that health results are purchasable — undermining the honest, evidence-based stance the practice sells (R-13's spirit applied to contracts). |
| Cash flow | Guaranteed revenue isn't earned revenue; a growing panel would need a refund reserve sized to a subjective liability — unmodelable. |

**Verdict: no outcomes or "value received" guarantee, in any form.**

## 2. What achieves the goal instead (the recommended structure)

Three mechanisms, all objective, all cheap, together delivering the intended
feeling:

1. **The free two-conversation consultation** (D-016) — the patient risks
   nothing to discover whether this is right. Already decided; it is the
   first and largest risk the practice takes.

2. **Defined engagement periods sized to uncertainty** (D-016/D-026) — an
   unsure fit gets a 3-month first period, not a year. "Try 3 months, then
   we both decide at a scheduled checkpoint with your own trend data on the
   table" *is* a money-back guarantee in structure — without a refund
   dispute, because the exit is a planned decision, not a claim.

3. **Automatic pro-rata refunds of unearned fees** — either party can end
   at (or between) checkpoints; months not yet delivered are refunded as a
   matter of course. The trigger is *time not delivered* — objective,
   administrable, dispute-free — not *value not perceived*. Patient-facing
   line: **"You never pay for care you haven't received."**

4. **One narrow satisfaction provision at the entry point:** if after the
   comprehensive evaluation the patient decides this isn't right, the
   evaluation fee is waived/refunded, no questions asked. Bounded (one
   moment, known cost, self-declared trigger), placed exactly where trust
   is scarcest (before the relationship exists), and small enough to be an
   acquisition cost rather than a liability. Patient-facing line: **"If
   the start doesn't feel right, the start is on me."**
   `NEEDS-COUNSEL` for wording; economically capped by design.

Together: *"The consultation costs nothing. If the start doesn't feel
right, the start is on me. We commit only to a defined period, we look at
your own data together at its end, and you never pay for care you haven't
received."* — That is the target feeling, achieved with zero subjective
triggers.

## 3. Upfront payment

**Findings:** upfront or staged payment is fine — and with pro-rata refunds
guaranteed, prepayment becomes trust-neutral for the patient (their
downside is bounded regardless of when they pay).

- **Default:** monthly card-on-file (familiarity — `payment-landscape.md`
  §2); staged start for front-loaded engagements (initial payment covering
  the evaluation phase, then monthly).
- **Full-period prepay:** offered, optionally with a modest prepay discount
  (🟦 common in DPC); never required.
- **Discipline that comes with it:** prepaid amounts are *deferred
  revenue* — refundable until earned. Operating rule: don't treat unearned
  cash as income; hold a simple reserve against refundable balances
  (a line in the economics model's overhead, and a `NEEDS-CPA` accounting
  question). Texas consumer-protection drafting for prepayment/refund
  terms: `NEEDS-COUNSEL` (DTPA).
- **GFE interplay:** the Schedule states the period price; it doubles as
  the self-pay Good Faith Estimate, so the prepaid amount and the estimate
  are the same number — no divergence to dispute.

## 4. What this closes and what stays open

Closed (D-027): no outcomes/value guarantee; risk-sharing via periods +
pro-rata refunds + entry satisfaction provision; prepayment optional, never
required; refund trigger is always objective.

Open: counsel wording for the satisfaction provision and refund terms;
reserve sizing (`NEEDS-CPA`); whether the entry provision applies to every
archetype or only relationship-shaped ones (an E-episode's evaluation *is*
much of the deliverable — likely excluded or halved; decide at template
finalization).
