# Scope & Fee Schedule — Template

*The individualized layer of the agreement architecture (D-026). One to two
pages, plain language, generated from the proposal process (D-016). The
patient should be able to read it once and know: what are we working on,
what am I getting, what's expected of both of us, how long are we trying
this, and what does it cost.*

*Draft — patient-facing use only after counsel review of the template and
the standard agreement it attaches to (`NEEDS-COUNSEL`). For self-pay
patients this document is designed to satisfy the Good Faith Estimate duty
(45 CFR 149.610 — `payment-landscape.md` §1.6).*

---

## The template

> ## Care Plan — Scope & Fee Schedule
> **Patient:** ___ · **Date:** ___ · **Attached to Practice Agreement v___**
>
> ### What we're working on
> *(2–4 sentences in plain words: the problems and goals this engagement
> addresses — written from what I heard, so the patient recognizes
> themselves in it.)*
>
> ### What I'm agreeing to manage
> *(The clinical scope: named conditions/questions. And the line: other
> issues that arise get honest triage — small things absorbed, significant
> new problems discussed openly as a scope change.)*
>
> ### How care will work
> - Engagement period: **___** (e.g., 12 months), beginning ___
> - Checkpoint(s): **___** (e.g., months 3 and 6) — where we review
>   progress, actual care needs, and whether this arrangement still fits
> - Expected rhythm: *(e.g., "visits every 2–3 weeks for the first three
>   months, then monthly — mostly by video, with home visits when
>   examination matters")*
> - Between visits: *(messaging expectations and response times, from the
>   standard agreement, restated in one line)*
> - Estimated physician time this period: **~___ hours** *(the basis of
>   the price — stated so the price has a visible derivation)*
>
> ### What we'll track
> *(The specific trends for this relationship — clinical measures plus
> well-being — visible to the patient, per D-023.)*
>
> ### The economics
> - Price for this period: **$___**, payable ___ *(e.g., $X/month by card
>   on file · or staged: $Y at start, then $X/month)*
> - What the price includes: everything described above
> - What it does not include: labs, imaging, medications, specialists,
>   urgent/emergency care — *(and how those run: through your insurance,
>   as now)*
> - Insurance expectations for this arrangement: *(private-pay: "I do not
>   bill your insurance; superbills available on request" · hybrid, if
>   ever verified permissible: what gets billed vs. what the fee covers)*
> - If we end early: unused time is refunded pro-rata — you never pay for
>   care you haven't received *(per the standard agreement's refund terms)*
>
> ### Specific to this relationship
> *(Exclusions or limits particular to this patient: e.g., psychiatric
> medication management remains with Dr. ___; out-of-state travel
> coverage limits; anything honest and specific.)*
>
> **Agreed:** patient ___ · physician ___ *(signatures/dates)*

## Worked example — "Sarah," the signature whole-life patient

*(Fictional composite. Illustrative pricing at the $210/hr scenario rate;
see unit economics §6 for what the rate must actually be.)*

> **What we're working on.** It's not one thing — it's everything at once:
> blood pressure creeping up, prediabetes, weight, broken sleep, more
> alcohol than you intend, and underneath it a stress load from work and
> the money strain at home. The goal of this year is to untangle it: treat
> what needs treating, work the rest in order, and get you back to feeling
> like your life is manageable.
>
> **What I'm agreeing to manage.** Your blood pressure and prediabetes;
> the sleep, alcohol, and weight work; the whole-picture plan and its
> priorities, including coordinating a counselor for the stress and a
> qualified financial professional for the money pressure — I'll help you
> think clearly about those as health matters, and connect you well, but
> I don't replace either. New significant problems get honest triage and
> an open conversation about scope.
>
> **How care will work.** 12 months from [date]. Checkpoints at month 3
> and month 6. Extended evaluation (~2 hours, split over two sittings),
> then visits every 2 weeks for ~3 months, then monthly — by video, with
> an in-person exam early on. Messaging anytime; I respond within 1–2
> business days, sooner when it matters. Estimated physician time:
> **~18–19 hours** across the year, weighted to the first three months.
>
> **What we'll track.** BP, glucose, weight, sleep, alcohol — and, just as
> seriously: stress, energy, mood, and your own 1–10 "how manageable is
> life" rating. Plus what you've spent. In a year, one page should tell
> us both whether this worked.
>
> **The economics.** **$4,700 for the year — $392/month by card on file**
> (or quarterly). Includes everything above. Doesn't include labs,
> medications, specialists, therapy itself, or financial advice itself —
> those run through your insurance and the professionals we bring in. I
> don't bill your insurance for this arrangement; superbills on request.
> If we stop early, unused months are refunded — you never pay for care
> you haven't received.
>
> **Specific to this relationship.** If the alcohol work turns out to need
> specialized treatment, that's a referral and a scope conversation, not
> something I'll manage alone past its safe point.

## Notes for building these (internal)

- The **estimated hours line is load-bearing** — it is the price's public
  derivation (D-002) and the checkpoint's yardstick (estimate vs. actual,
  R-08). Never omit it.
- Prices come from the internal model (time × complexity-adjusted rate ×
  (1 + uncertainty allowance) + direct resources), then the value check.
  The patient sees the fixed price and the hours basis — **not** an hourly
  meter; see unit economics §2 for why fixed-price-outside/time-inside
  beats billing hours.
- Renewal Schedules can be half this length: scope deltas, new period, new
  checkpoint, new price.
- The archetype scenario cards (`docs/strategy/patient-archetypes.md`)
  are the starting priors for the hours estimate — tailoring is
  configuration, not invention (R-20).
