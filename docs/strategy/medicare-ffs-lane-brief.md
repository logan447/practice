# Path B Brief — Billing Medicare FFS Through the PLLC

*Research brief 2026-08-22 (background agent, primary sources cited).
Question: instead of excluding Medicare beneficiaries, can DuBose
Medical Services, PLLC bill Medicare fee-for-service for Medicare
patients (no membership fee) alongside the $100/mo membership for
non-Medicare adults — while Dr. DuBose stays enrolled and employed at
FCHC? Labels: VERIFIED / LIKELY / EST / NEEDS-MAC-CONFIRMATION /
NEEDS-COUNSEL / NEEDS-BILLER-QUOTE. All 2026 rates REFRESH-AT-RUNWAY.*

## 1. Enrollment mechanics — routine and free

- **Concurrent affiliations are permitted.** `VERIFIED` A physician
  may hold multiple simultaneous reassignments/practice affiliations;
  since CMS-855R was retired (Oct 2023) reassignments live on the
  consolidated CMS-855I. Keeping the FCHC reassignment while billing
  through his own PLLC is routine.
- **Sole-owner-PLLC path: 855I only.** `VERIFIED` The physician
  enrolls the PLLC through his own 855I ("sole owner of a
  professional entity"); the PLLC gets its own PTAN as billing
  entity; no CMS-855B unless it becomes a multi-practitioner group.
  Prereqs: PLLC formed, EIN, **Type 2 organizational NPI**, bank
  account (CMS-588 EFT).
- **No application fee** for physicians/physician organizations.
  `VERIFIED` (fee applies to institutional providers only).
- **Timing:** PECOS filing; MAC (Palmetto GBA JM for Virginia)
  processing ~30–90 days; effective date backdates to filing with a
  30-day retrospective-billing window (42 CFR 424.520(d)/.521).
- **Home-based/house-call practice location:** workable — home
  address as administrative location with services in patients'
  homes. `LIKELY`; `NEEDS-MAC-CONFIRMATION` before filing (CY2026
  rule tightened telehealth-address flexibility). No P.O. boxes.

## 2. Participation (CMS-460)

Participation binds per billing entity/TIN; the **new PLLC gets its
own election window** (with enrollment or within 90 days of
approval). Whether his existing individual CMS-460 (if any, via FCHC)
auto-binds the PLLC: `NEEDS-MAC-CONFIRMATION` in writing. Non-par
(95% allowed, 115% limiting charge = 109.25% ceiling, collect from
patient) is **not worth it at micro scale** — recommend
participating. `EST/judgment`

## 3. What Medicare pays (CY2026, national non-facility)

Conversion factor $33.40 non-QP (+~3.3%). Key rates: 99213 ~$90;
**99214 $135.61**; 99215 ~$186; **G2211 $16.57** (from 2026 also
billable with home-visit codes); AWV G0439 $137.61; home visits
99347–99350 ~$80–$193; **CCM 99490 $66.30/mo**, 99439 $50.56, 99491
~$89; messaging 99421–23 ~$16–51/7-day episode. Part B deductible
$283 (2026); patient/Medigap pays 20% coinsurance.

**Telehealth: funded.** After the Sept-2025 cliff, the Consolidated
Appropriations Act 2026 (signed Feb 3, 2026) extended Medicare
telehealth flexibilities **through Dec 31, 2027**: home as
originating site, no geographic restriction; audio-only payable when
the patient can't/won't use video. `VERIFIED` Next cliff: Jan 1, 2028.

**Per-patient arithmetic:** complex patient (8 visits + G2211 + AWV +
12 mo CCM) ≈ **$2,251/yr allowed**; moderate (4 visits + AWV) ≈ $746;
conservative planning case (6 × 99214 + G2211 + AWV) ≈ **$1,051/yr**
— vs $1,200/yr membership. Comparable revenue, more admin.

## 4. Does FFS constrain the practice style? Mostly no.

No per-patient visit caps; the constraint is documented medical
necessity. Time-based E/M billing rewards long visits (G2212 for very
long). **No homebound requirement for physician home visits**
(homebound belongs to home health, not physician E/M) — document why
the home. Telehealth essentially unrestricted through 2027; several
frequency limits removed in CY2026. Messaging is clinically
unlimited; Medicare just pays only defined slices (patient-initiated,
7-day episodes). **Plain answer: Medicare changes how you get paid
and documents, not how you practice.** The binding constraints are
financial (§5).

## 5. What he cannot charge Medicare patients

Collect deductible + 20% coinsurance; **cannot routinely waive**
cost-sharing (AKS/inducement; documented hardship only,
`NEEDS-COUNSEL`); **cannot charge any membership/access fee touching
covered services** (2004 OIG alert: "coordination of care,"
"assessment and plan," "extra time" are covered). Legitimate extras:
no-show fees (all patients equally), truly non-covered services.
Consensus: a small non-covered-amenities fee is **not worth the
compliance risk** at micro scale — Medicare patients pay nothing
beyond cost-sharing.

## 6. Admin burden (5–20 Medicare patients)

- **MIPS: exempt.** `VERIFIED` 2026 low-volume threshold (must exceed
  ALL of >$90k allowed charges / >200 beneficiaries / >200 services).
- Claims: free MAC channels or EHR-integrated clearinghouse
  (~$75–150/mo) or billing service ~4–8% of collections
  (`NEEDS-BILLER-QUOTE`).
- MSP questionnaires; fall par-election + fee-schedule refresh;
  occasional denials; TPE audit exposure low for a low-volume clean
  biller.
- **Realistic load: setup 10–20 hr once; steady state 3–6 hr/mo DIY
  or ~1 hr/mo + 4–8% with a biller.** `EST`

## 7. Hybrid practice (members + Medicare FFS) is lawful

No rule prohibits one practice serving cash members (non-Medicare)
and FFS Medicare patients — the classic hybrid model (GAO-05-929).
Cautions: keep membership agreements strictly to the non-Medicare
lane (Va. Code §54.1-2998 disclosures); **two-class-service design
risk** — if members visibly get better access than Medicare patients,
it recreates the OIG "selling access to covered services" concern.
Design so Medicare patients get substantially the same clinical
access; membership prices the payment arrangement, not a better
doctor. `NEEDS-COUNSEL` (service-tier design memo).

## 8. Medicare Advantage

Out-of-network MA is payable at original-Medicare rates (42 CFR
422.214) but per-plan billing, HMO denials, slow PPO payment.
**Recommendation: traditional-Medicare-only lane at launch; MA PPO
case-by-case later.**
