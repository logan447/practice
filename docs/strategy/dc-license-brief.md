# DC Licensure Brief — Pressure Test

*Research brief 2026-08-22 (background agent, primary sources cited).
Question: does early DC licensure justify its cost during months
0–12? Labels: VERIFIED / LIKELY / EST / UNCLEAR / NEEDS-COUNSEL /
NEEDS-CPA / NEEDS-BROKER.*

## Bottom line

**The DC license is not an optional market expansion — it is close to
a compliance prerequisite for the home-office model he already has.
File now.** He lives and will practice from DC (20024).

## 1. What it unlocks / doesn't

Unlocks: telemedicine to DC-located patients, DC house calls,
marketing to DC. DC Health guidance and Board Policy 15-01:
practice occurs where the patient is; treating DC-located patients
requires DC licensure (narrow §3-1205.02 exemptions only).
`VERIFIED` Market: DC ~560–580k adults vs NoVa ~4× larger, but his
neighborhood and warm network are disproportionately DC. Does NOT
unlock Maryland (own license), controlled-substance prescribing
(separate DC CSR + DEA anchoring), or entity/tax registrations.

## 2. The sitting-in-DC question (the crux)

- **Virginia's position — worse than assumed.** `VERIFIED` VA BOM
  Guidance 90-64: practitioner must be licensed by the state where
  the patient is located **and the state where the practitioner is
  located**. Virginia's own board expects a DC license for a
  physician sitting in DC, even treating VA patients. (Guidance, not
  statute — `NEEDS-COUNSEL` on enforceability.)
- **DC's position — probably permissive, unresolved.** `UNCLEAR` DC's
  2020 telehealth guidance implies out-bound telemedicine from DC
  isn't "practice in the District," but no authority squarely says so.
- **Insurers already price the ambiguity:** the Core Specialty quote
  excludes DC-situs services including telemedicine.
- **Prescribing.** `VERIFIED` DEA registration follows the
  **patient's** state (DEA-DC-044); a VA-anchored DEA covers VA
  patients wherever he sits. But the registered address must be a
  bona fide principal practice location (21 CFR 1301.12); once solo,
  a DC-home-anchored DEA requires DC license + DC CSR ($130
  biennial) first, and a home registration becomes inspectable
  "controlled premises" (DEA-DC-052). Telemedicine flexibilities run
  through Dec 31, 2026; whether the separate-registration exception
  survives 2027 is `UNCLEAR` — plan as if patient-state registration
  is required from 2027. `NEEDS-COUNSEL`.

## 3. Home visits and home office in DC

House calls: DC home-care-agency licensure (§44-501) doesn't fit a
solo physician (`LIKELY`; low-risk `NEEDS-COUNSEL`). Home office:
Home Occupation Permit `LIKELY` required (~$123 all-in, one-time).

## 4. Entity + tax

- FN-1 foreign registration of the VA PLLC in DC: **$220** + biennial
  BRA-25 $300 + DC registered agent ~$125/yr. `VERIFIED` (verify a VA
  *P*LLC's DC qualification — `NEEDS-COUNSEL`).
- **DC UBT: exempt.** `VERIFIED text` §47-1808.01 excludes >80%
  personal-services businesses; solo physician fits. Filing mechanics
  (D-30 above $12k gross claiming exemption): `NEEDS-CPA`.
- **DC resident income tax kills the "Virginia tax home" framing:**
  all practice profit lands on his DC return regardless. VA
  nonresident treatment of pass-through income + DC credit:
  `NEEDS-CPA`. Tax-neutral to the license decision.
- BBL: `LIKELY` exempt (health-occupations licensure).

## 5. Malpractice

DC has **no damages cap** (VA caps ~$2.65M) → expect a DC surcharge.
`EST` +$1,000–3,000/yr. Broker questions: endorse DC as covered
territory for patient-location AND physician-location; admitted
carriers writing DC solo IM/DPC (MedPro, TDC, Curi); house-call
rating. `NEEDS-BROKER`.

## 6. Value case, months 0–12

Carrying cost ≈ **$600–700/yr** (renewal ~$250–340/yr + CSR $65/yr +
BRA-25 $150/yr + agent ~$125/yr) + malpractice delta. Each DC member
nets ~$96/mo ≈ $1,150/yr → **one member covers baseline carrying
cost; 2–4 cover it with the malpractice surcharge.** First-year
all-in ≈ **$1,500** ($805 app + ~$100 verifications + CSR $130 +
FN-1 $220 + agent + HOP); +$888/3yr if a DC-anchored DEA is added.
CME: 50 hr/2-yr cycle (first renewal CE-exempt; specific required
hours). `LIKELY`

## 7. Texas move

No in-District presence requirement found for holding/renewing the DC
license (`LIKELY`; confirm at renewal). From Texas: keep DC patients
by telemedicine like VA patients; ~$550–700/yr more than VA-only.
DEA anchor moves to TX when principal practice moves.

## 8. Timing

2–4 months, no expedite; verifications start only after filing.
**File now** → license ~Nov 2026–Jan 2027, at or before launch.
Filing at Stage 2 pushes DC legality and the home-office/malpractice
fix into mid-2027.

## Recommendation

**File now.** Deciding fact: he lives and practices from DC; VA's own
guidance expects licensure where the practitioner sits; DC's outbound
position is unresolved; his malpractice quote already excludes
DC-situs care. The license converts the home office from a gray zone
into a lawful, insurable principal place of practice, unlocks the
densest warm network, and costs ~1 member/yr to carry.
