## A. Medicare — how the rules actually attach

1. **Enrollment is individual.** `VERIFIED` Physician enrollment rides
   the individual NPI (CMS-855I in PECOS); FCHC bills via a
   *reassignment* of his benefits (855R function, now merged into
   855I). Reassignment lets FCHC collect for his services; it does not
   make the enrollment FCHC's. If a CMS-460 (participation agreement)
   is on file it binds the NPI for **all** his Part B services
   anywhere. `OWNER-FACT`: check PECOS for enrollment, CMS-460, active
   reassignments.
2. **Mandatory claim submission is the core prohibition.** `VERIFIED`
   SSA §1848(g)(4): every Medicare-covered service furnished to a
   beneficiary must be billed to Medicare — this applies to any
   enrolled-and-not-opted-out (even never-enrolled) physician, at any
   location. "We just won't bill Medicare, the member pays cash" is
   not lawful for covered services. CMPs up to $2,000/violation.
3. **Nearly the whole $100/mo bundle is "covered services."**
   `VERIFIED` per-element: office E/M covered; telehealth mostly
   covered; home visits covered (99341–50); chronic care management /
   care planning / coordination covered (CCM codes; the 2004 OIG alert
   specifically treated "coordination of care" and "assessment and
   plan" as covered); portal messaging largely billable (99421–23).
   Genuinely non-covered: routine annual physical beyond the AWV,
   24/7-access amenities, same-day guarantees. `LIKELY`: the DPC fee
   cannot be re-labeled non-covered without gutting it.
4. **The three legitimate lanes** (no fourth exists):
   - **A. Exclude Medicare beneficiaries (incl. Medicare Advantage)
     from membership.** Lawful and the standard hybrid-physician
     answer (`LIKELY`; no obligation to accept Medicare patients; a
     practice billing no federal program takes no federal financial
     assistance). Screen by insurance status, never by age. The trap
     is a member **aging into** Medicare: the agreement needs a
     termination/conversion clause. `NEEDS-COUNSEL` (clause drafting).
   - **B. Concierge lane (MDVIP model): membership covers ONLY
     genuinely non-covered items; Medicare is billed normally for
     covered care.** Real but narrow and audit-exposed (OIG 2004
     alert; Rocamora settlement ~$106,600). Requires him to enroll and
     bill in the private practice — infrastructure and posture that
     defeat the simple $100 model. Keep as a documented non-option.
   - **C. Opt out + private contracts (42 CFR 405.415).** Opt-out is
     all-or-nothing per physician, all locations (MBPM Ch. 15 §40.23);
     after opt-out **no entity may be paid by Medicare for his
     services**, including FCHC under reassignment (§40.30, 42 CFR
     405.425); MACs are directed to recover such payments. If FCHC is
     a true free clinic billing no Medicare for him, opt-out is
     operationally clean. If FCHC bills Part B: opt-out takes that
     revenue away → effectively blocked unless FCHC agrees. If FCHC is
     an FQHC/RHC (PPS/encounter-rate): **no exception found** allowing
     encounters by an opted-out physician; do not rely on the
     "not-paid-under-PFS" theory. `NEEDS-MAC-CONFIRMATION` (Palmetto
     GBA) + `NEEDS-COUNSEL`. Mechanics if ever used: affidavit to the
     MAC 30 days before a quarterly date; 2-year auto-renewing terms;
     emergency/urgent care to non-contracted beneficiaries must still
     be billed (GJ modifier, limiting charge).
5. **Medicare Advantage follows the same logic.** `VERIFIED` 42 CFR
   422.214 applies original-Medicare rules (incl. penalties) to
   non-contract providers serving MA enrollees; treat MA members
   identically in the membership screen. MA cards don't look like
   Medicare cards — screening must ask explicitly.
6. **Duals/QMB.** `VERIFIED` Without opt-out, charging QMBs anything
   for covered services is flatly prohibited. With opt-out + private
   contract, CMS's Jan-2025 FAQ says QMB protections don't apply to
   the (non-covered) private-contract services — BUT while he holds a
   Virginia Medicaid enrollment, Medicaid billing rules still reach
   Medicaid-covered services. Operating rule regardless of lane:
   **duals/QMBs are not enrolled in paid membership.** `NEEDS-COUNSEL`
   if that ever changes.

## B. Medicaid (Virginia DMAS) — separate rules, stricter text

1. **Enrollment is (almost certainly) individual here too.**
   `VERIFIED` DMAS enrolls rendering practitioners individually with
   their own participation agreements (Practitioner Manual Ch. II);
   if FCHC bills Medicaid for him, he likely signed one. Could be
   full or ORP-only (ordering/referring/prescribing).
   `NEEDS-DMAS-CONFIRMATION` via MES/Gainwell portal.
2. **Virginia's member-billing bar is categorical for covered
   services.** `VERIFIED` Ch. II: a provider "may not bill an eligible
   individual for a covered service regardless of whether the provider
   received payment from the state." The Ch. I private-pay exception
   (advance written, signed, dated consent; "accepted as a private
   pay patient") clearly covers **non-covered or MCO-denied
   services**; whether it lets a participating physician privately
   contract for routine covered primary care is unresolved and the
   Ch. II sentence cuts against it. `NEEDS-COUNSEL` +
   `NEEDS-DMAS-CONFIRMATION` (in writing) before ever trying.
3. **No opt-out analog exists in Medicaid.** `VERIFIED` The lever is
   the participation agreement itself; he could terminate individually
   on 30 days' notice, but FCHC would lose Medicaid billing for his
   services and his orders/referrals would stop being honored —
   harmful to the clinic. Not worth it while employed there.
4. **Managed care (Cardinal Care MCOs)** adds nothing for a
   never-billing cash practice beyond the same member-billing rule.
   `VERIFIED`
5. **Virginia's DPC statute (§§54.1-2997/2998) is silent** on
   Medicare/Medicaid beneficiaries — it neither permits nor forbids
   enrolling them. `VERIFIED`

## C. Operating posture adopted for planning (pending counsel)

While employed at FCHC and enrolled in both programs:

- Membership is open to adults **not** covered by Medicare (incl.
  MA) or Medicaid; screening happens at enrollment by insurance
  status, with an aging-in/conversion clause. `NEEDS-COUNSEL` for the
  clause and screening language.
- Duals/QMBs: never enrolled in paid membership, under any structure.
- Free/charity care to anyone raises no payment-in-full issue and
  stays available at the physician's discretion.
- Lane C (opt-out) is re-evaluated only on an FCHC-facts change
  (free-clinic billing status confirmed, or employment ends) — it is
  the pure-DPC end state, not the launch state.

## D. Confirmations queue

1. `OWNER-FACT` PECOS pull: enrollment status, CMS-460 yes/no, active
   reassignments. (Own login; 20 minutes.)
2. `OWNER-FACT` FCHC billing manager: does FCHC bill Medicare for his
   encounters, under what method (Part B PFS vs FQHC PPS vs nothing)?
   Same question for Medicaid. Written answer.
3. `NEEDS-DMAS-CONFIRMATION` His individual DMAS status (full vs ORP)
   via Gainwell; written DMAS answer on the private-pay question only
   if lane ever matters.
4. `NEEDS-MAC-CONFIRMATION` Only if opt-out becomes live and FCHC is
   FQHC-like: Palmetto GBA on opted-out-physician encounters.
5. `NEEDS-COUNSEL` Membership agreement: Medicare/MA/Medicaid/QMB
   screening reps, aging-in clause; confirm posture C collision
   analysis.
