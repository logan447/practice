# Channel Map

*2026-08-21. Every meaningful channel, its job in the funnel, and its
launch priority. §1 is the master table; §2 holds full profiles for the
priority channels (the 13-attribute capture); §3 specs the referral
packet. Cost and time figures are planning priors — labeled, and
replaced by ledger actuals (`measurement.md`) as they exist. Ratings:
`●●●` high · `●●` medium · `●` low.*

**Priority key:** `P1` = build/activate for launch · `P2` = activate by
~25 patients · `P3` = activate by ~50 / mature · `T` = bounded test ·
`—` = deliberately deprioritized (reason given).

## 2. Priority-channel profiles (full 13-attribute capture)

### A1 — Google Business Profile + Maps

- **Target audience:** anyone in the metro searching "doctor near me,"
  "primary care Austin," or the practice's name (every other channel
  drives name searches — GBP is where they land).
- **Funnel stage:** TOF discovery; also the reputation surface (reviews).
- **Patient intent:** high — active seekers.
- **Message:** category + services + the one-line practice description
  ($100/month membership, unhurried primary care, home visits when
  indicated, video-first); photos of a real human, not stock (design §7).
- **CTA:** website link → book page; booking link field → scheduler.
- **Cost:** $0.
- **Physician time:** ~1 h setup; ~15 min/wk (posts optional, Q&A,
  review responses).
- **Time to results:** listing live in weeks; map-pack competitiveness
  builds with review count/velocity over months.
- **Measurement:** GBP insights (calls, direction/website clicks) +
  ledger "found via Google/Maps" + booking-source answers.
- **Scalability:** capped by geography — which matches the practice.
- **Priority:** P1 at launch. **Dependencies:** operating address.
  Home-visit model may fit GBP's *service-area business* rules
  (address hidden, service area declared) — verify current rules at
  setup; misclassification risks suspension *(unverified —
  check at runway)*.
- **Risks/compliance:** review solicitation must be un-incentivized and
  un-gated (`compliance.md` §4); review *responses* must never confirm
  patient status (HIPAA); listing suspensions are common for
  service-area businesses — follow rules exactly.

### A2/A3 — Website SEO + the education library

- **Target audience:** Tier 2 (DPC-aware searchers), Tier 3
  (home-visit seekers), Tier 4 (question-askers) per `funnel.md` §1.1.
- **Funnel stage:** TOF; essays double as MOF trust assets.
- **Patient intent:** Tier 2/3 high; Tier 4 mixed.
- **Message:** the six pillars (design §5); essays in the sense-making
  clinical voice.
- **CTA:** the one CTA, plus letter signup on essays.
- **Cost:** $0 beyond existing site.
- **Physician time:** the real spend — ~3–4 h/essay *(prior)*;
  Era-1 target: the 12-essay cornerstone library before launch, ~1–2
  essays/month within Track B pacing.
- **Time to results:** 6–18 months post-publication in-market;
  compounds.
- **Measurement:** Search Console queries/positions; Plausible organic
  sessions reaching /book; ledger "found you on Google / read your
  article."
- **Scalability:** high — works while sleeping; the only channel that
  does at this practice's scale.
- **Priority:** P1 — the defining Era-1 investment.
- **Dependencies:** domain purchased now (age helps); two site
  additions fed back to Track B: a **home-visits page** and a
  **panel-status/accepting-new-patients page**.
- **Risks/compliance:** R-13 and credential guardrails apply to every
  essay; educational content stays general (no individualized advice);
  TMB-advertising counsel pass before publication wave-one.

**Cornerstone library — working list (12):**
1. What direct primary care is — and what it isn't *(Tier 2 anchor)*
2. How this practice works with Medicare (opt-out, private contracts, in plain words — D-033's public voice)
3. How this practice works alongside Medicaid and regular insurance
4. What $100/month covers, what it doesn't, and why the price is public
5. House calls in 2026+: what a home visit is actually for *(Tier 3 anchor)*
6. DPC vs. concierge vs. insurance clinic: an honest comparison (incl. cost)
7. How to read your lab results (and what "normal" doesn't rule out)
8. Five specialists, no quarterback: whole-picture primary care
9. What a good first visit looks like when the doctor has time
10. Preventive care that's actually evidence-based (and what's marketing)
11. When you should NOT join a practice like this *(the trust essay)*
12. Making sense of a new diagnosis: a working method

### B1–B3 — Professional referral sources (specialists · therapists · pharmacists)

- **Target audience:** the professionals themselves; through them, their
  frustrated/unmanaged patients and clients.
- **Funnel stage:** TOF via Engine B; referred people enter near MEET
  with borrowed trust.
- **Patient intent:** very high — a trusted professional told them to go.
- **Message (per source type, funnel.md §5):** specialists — workups
  before, follow-through after, notes actually read; therapists —
  a medical partner for whole-person care; pharmacists — someone to own
  the medication list.
- **CTA:** "hand them this packet; they book a free conversation; I'll
  be honest about fit and refer onward if I'm not it."
- **Cost:** packet printing (~$2–5/packet *prior*).
- **Physician time:** the dominant cost — visit ~1–1.5 h with travel
  *(prior)*; plus report-backs. Ramp budget: ~4 h/wk (phased plan).
- **Time to results:** first referral typically 1–6 months into a
  relationship *(prior)*; habit forms only after a visibly well-handled
  first referral.
- **Measurement:** ledger source list — per source: contacts made,
  packets left, referrals received, consults, enrollments, hours spent.
- **Scalability:** medium — bounded by relationship maintenance; the
  mature engine target is ~10–15 *active* sources, not 100 contacts.
- **Priority:** P1 — the primary engine for patients ~5–50.
- **Dependencies:** the packet (§3); authorization form for
  report-backs; enough schedule slack to see referred people fast.
- **Risks/compliance:** zero remuneration either direction (Tex. Occ.
  Code ch. 102 — `compliance.md` §3); therapist-heavy inflow skews
  panel heavy (R-26 — balance, don't stop); report-backs require
  patient authorization (HIPAA).

### B9 — Community talks

- **Audience:** libraries, churches, senior centers, employers (later);
  attendees skew exactly toward people frustrated with rushed care.
- **Stage:** TOF + MOF simultaneously (they meet the physician's manner
  live — the strongest trust transfer short of a referral).
- **Intent:** medium walking in; high walking out when it resonates.
- **Message:** genuinely useful health education (talk titles:
  "How to get more out of your doctor visits," "Making sense of your
  labs," "Managing five conditions without losing the plot"); the
  practice gets 90 seconds at the end, not the podium.
- **CTA:** letter signup sheet + explainer one-pagers on the back table.
- **Cost:** ~$0. **Physician time:** ~3 h per event incl. prep-reuse
  *(prior)*.
- **Time to results:** immediate trickle per event; compounds via
  repeat invitations.
- **Measurement:** attendees (count), signups, ledger source tag.
- **Scalability:** low-medium; one talk/month is plenty at maturity.
- **Priority:** P1 (first talks can even predate launch as validation).
- **Dependencies:** one polished talk deck; the signup mechanism.
- **Risks/compliance:** educational-not-solicitation register at
  senior/Medicaid-adjacent venues (inducement optics, Q-21);
  R-13 discipline live and unscripted — practice the boundaries.

### C1 — The letter (newsletter / founding interest list)

Profile in `funnel.md` §2. Attributes summary: audience = interested,
not ready; stage = MOF; intent = medium, warming; cost ≈ $0–15/mo tool
*(typical indie tier, unverified)*; MD time ~2 h/issue; results = slow
build, then material ("been reading you for months" consults);
measurement = list growth, letter-attributed consults; scalability =
high; priority = **P1 and the only channel that starts at full speed in

### A12 — Google Search ads (the launch test)

Audience = Tier 2/3 searchers before organic matures; stage = TOF;
intent = the highest purchasable; message = plain text ads, price
included ("Unhurried primary care — $100/mo, no insurance needed");
CTA = book page; cost = bounded test ~$300–500/mo × 3 mo *(planning
band, not a quote)*; MD time = low after setup; results = days;
measurement = cost per consult and per enrollee from ledger (ad click →
"found via Google ad" asked at booking — no conversion pixels beyond
Google's own click reporting, consistent with the no-surveillance
posture); scalability = medium, budget-linear; priority = **T at
launch**; dependencies = GBP live, book path frictionless;
risks/compliance = health personalization restrictions (no
remarketing/audience targeting — search ads on keywords are the
compliant shape, `compliance.md` §6); never advertise controlled
substances or specific weight-loss medications (policy + R-26 demand
magnets).

## 3. The referral packet — spec (the physical outreach artifact)

*The packet already in design belongs to this workstream; this is its
requirements spec. Owner: Track B design + this workstream.*

**For the professional (stays at their desk):**
- One page: who I am, what I take off your plate, exactly what happens
  when you send someone (fast booking, honest fit answer, onward
  referral if not me, note back with authorization), how to reach me
  directly (the professional back-channel — a real differentiator).
- Credential accuracy per the standing guardrails; licensure stated
  plainly.

**For the person they hand it to (give-away, ~5 copies/packet):**
- The shareable explainer (C2): what this is in two minutes, the price,
  who it's for and isn't for, the free-conversation CTA with de-risking
  subtext, the URL/QR to the book page.

**Register:** matches the website — calm, plain, no coupon-feel, no
"special offer." **Explicitly absent:** any referral form that captures
the prospective patient's health information on paper (no-PHI rule
extends to print), any incentive language, any outcome claims (R-13).

**Measurement hook:** packets carry no tracking codes (register cost >
data value at this scale); attribution comes from asking at booking —
"Dr. ___ / the pharmacist at ___ sent me" is exactly the granularity
the source ledger needs.
