# Archetype S — Stable Chronic Disease

**Who:** Fictional composite — "Maria," 58, hypertension and well-controlled
type 2 diabetes on stable medications. Managed, but managed *thinly*: 15-minute
visits, refills that lapse, labs nobody explains. Wants her conditions
genuinely tended and her medication burden honestly reviewed.

**Engagement shape:** ongoing, moderate. Quarterly rhythm, annual deep
evaluation.

---

## The proposal Maria would see

> ### Care Proposal — prepared for Maria · [date]
>
> **What I heard.** Your blood pressure and diabetes are "fine" on paper,
> but nobody has stepped back in years to ask whether the whole picture —
> five medications, your energy, your kidneys, your actual goals — still
> makes sense. Refills and lab results feel like paperwork, not care.
>
> **What I believe I can help with.** Keeping both conditions genuinely
> controlled; a real medication review with simplification where safe;
> catching kidney, eye, heart, and nerve complications early; making labs
> and numbers something you understand rather than receive.
>
> **The plan.**
> - **Comprehensive annual evaluation** (~90 min, in person): everything —
>   history, exam, complication screening review, full medication
>   reconciliation, goals.
> - **Quarterly reviews** (~30–40 min, telemedicine): BP and glucose
>   trends, labs twice yearly reviewed together, adjustments.
> - **Between visits:** message me anytime; refills handled without
>   games — renewed at visits so you never chase them; results explained
>   within 1–2 business days.
> - **If things wobble** (a stretch of high readings, a new symptom): we
>   add visits as needed — that flexibility is built into the price, within
>   reason, and we're honest with each other if "wobble" becomes "new
>   phase."
>
> **What we'll track:** BP trend, A1c, kidney function, lipids, weight,
> medication count and doses, symptoms, and how you're actually feeling —
> visible to you as trends, alongside what you've spent.
>
> **Duration & checkpoints.** One year at a time; renewal decided together
> at the annual evaluation, looking at the trends and the cost.
>
> **Included:** all visits above, messaging, results interpretation, refill
> management, coordination with your eye doctor/other specialists, records.
> **Not included:** labs, imaging, medications themselves, specialists,
> urgent care/ER (all continue through your insurance as now); after-hours
> coverage; management of significant *new* conditions — those trigger an
> honest re-scoping conversation, not silent scope creep.
>
> **The economics.** **~$2,200/year** — about $185/month. That reflects
> roughly 10–11 hours of my time on your care across the year at the
> practice's standard rate. You'll always know where we stand: if your
> year runs heavier or lighter than planned, the renewal price follows
> reality, in both directions.

## Money flow — patient's perspective

**Scenario 1 · Private-pay (default):** ~$185/mo card on file (or
$2,200/yr). Insurance continues to cover labs, medications, specialists,
acute care elsewhere. Total out-of-pocket to me: ~$2,200/yr, no surprises.

**Scenario 2 · Hybrid (researched 2026-08; counsel still required):** Maria
is the patient for whom the hybrid is most attractive — chronic disease
management is squarely covered care, and commercial reimbursement for her
~5 visits (primary care E/M runs ~110–130% of Medicare; a 99214 ≈ $136
Medicare-national) could offset a substantially reduced private fee. **But
the research sharpened the constraint** (`payment-landscape.md` §1): the
private fee may not cover anything the payer considers part of covered
services — and "extended time, messaging, coordination" is exactly the
inventory the 2004 OIG Alert flagged and in-network hold-harmless clauses
guard. A compliant version exists (the MDVIP pattern: fee buys a genuinely
non-covered wellness layer) but it constrains what the fee can honestly
describe. If Maria is Medicare-age, add the mandatory-claims rule and
fee-inventory limits in full. Contract-by-contract and counsel review
before any hybrid is offered (Q-15/X-04).

## Physician-side economics (illustrative, $210/hr scenario rate)

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$2,200/yr (1.0 × 10.5 hr × $210) |
| Hours: sync / async / admin | 5.0 / 4.0 / 1.5 = **10.5 hr** |
| Attributable costs | ~$65 card fees; 1 space session (UNSET) |
| Contribution (pre-space) | ~$2,140 → **~$204/attributable-hr** |
| Hybrid variant | insurance collections per visit + reduced private fee; net revenue similar or higher, but adds claims/coding admin time — the admin delta is the deciding variable (Q-02) |

**Two-sided test:** Maria pays ~$6/day for genuinely tended chronic disease
— against the reference point of thin 15-minute care, the value story is
strong *if she can afford it*; she is also the archetype where the hybrid,
if permissible, most reduces the affordability barrier. Physician earns at
target rate with predictable, plannable work. **Passes; hybrid question
matters most here.**
