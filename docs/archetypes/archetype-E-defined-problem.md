# Archetype E — Defined-Problem Episode

**Who:** Fictional composite — "Tom," 38, wants one thing solved: months of
reflux that over-the-counter medicine isn't fixing, plus he'd like to know
it isn't something worse. He does not want a membership, a program, or an
indefinite relationship. He wants a competent physician to take the problem
seriously, work it up properly, fix it, and be done.

**Engagement shape:** time-boxed episode (~2–3 months), clean ending, door
left open. **This archetype is why the practice explicitly does not require
membership** (D-014) — some people need an excellent episode, not a
relationship, and serving them well is both legitimate care and how
relationships sometimes begin.

---

## The proposal Tom would see

> ### Care Proposal — prepared for Tom · [date]
>
> **What I heard.** Reflux for four months, worse at night, antacids
> half-working, and a quiet worry in the back of your mind. You want it
> evaluated properly and solved — not a subscription.
>
> **What I believe I can help with.** Exactly that: a proper evaluation,
> honest triage of the worry (including whether anything warrants a
> scope), a real treatment plan, and follow-through until it's settled.
>
> **The plan.**
> - **Focused evaluation** (~45–60 min, telemedicine): full story, risk
>   assessment, examination in person if findings warrant, plan.
> - **Treatment trial** with the right medication at the right dose, plus
>   the two or three lifestyle changes that actually matter for reflux.
> - **Two follow-ups** (~20–30 min) over ~8–10 weeks: response check,
>   adjustment, and the referral decision (GI/endoscopy) if triggers are
>   met — coordinated by me with your insurance.
> - **Messaging** between visits for questions about the plan.
>
> **What we'll track:** symptom severity (a simple weekly score), triggers,
> medication response — so the endpoint is visible: settled, or escalated
> appropriately.
>
> **Duration & endpoint.** ~3 months, then we're done — with a written
> summary for your records and any future doctor. If it turns out to be
> bigger than reflux, I'll say so early and we re-scope or refer properly.
> If you later want ongoing care, you already know how I work.
>
> **Included:** evaluation, follow-ups, messaging within the episode,
> referral coordination, written summary.
> **Not included:** medications, testing, GI/endoscopy (insurance, as
> usual); anything outside this problem — I'll flag other findings honestly
> but treating them is a separate conversation.
>
> **The economics.** **~$1,500 for the episode**, flat — payable upfront or
> in two–three installments. Roughly 7–8 hours of my time across the
> episode. No membership, no renewal, no ongoing charge.

## Money flow — patient's perspective

**Scenario 1 · Private-pay (default):** flat episode price, upfront or
staged. The cleanest money story in the practice: one problem, one price,
one ending. (For self-pay arrangements a written Good Faith Estimate is a
likely *legal requirement*, not a courtesy — see `payment-landscape.md`.)

**Scenario 2 · Hybrid/insurance (conditional):** an episode like this maps
almost perfectly onto conventional insurance billing (3 E/M visits) —
meaning if credentialed, the *fully-insured* version of Tom is plausible:
he pays copays, insurance pays the visits, and there may be little room or
need for a private fee at all. **This archetype is therefore the live test
of a real strategic question: for defined-problem care, is the private
episode price competing against a $60 copay path?** The answer shapes who
this archetype is for (uninsured, high-deductible, out-of-network-by-choice
patients) — a Track F validation question, not an assumption.

## Physician-side economics (illustrative, $210/hr scenario rate)

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$1,500/episode (1.0 × 7.5 hr × $210, rounded) |
| Hours: sync / async / admin | 4 / 2.5 / 1 = **7.5 hr** |
| Attributable costs | ~$45 card fees |
| Contribution | ~$1,455 → **~$194/attributable-hr** |
| Practice role | fills capacity between longitudinal arrangements; low commitment; natural on-ramp — some E patients become P/S/U |

**Two-sided test:** Tom pays a known price for a solved problem and a
straight answer, without subscribing to anything. Physician earns near
target rate with a clean endpoint. **Passes — with the copay-competition
caveat above: this archetype's market is patients for whom the insurance
path is unavailable, exhausted, or not worth its friction, and the
proposal should be honest about that comparison.**
