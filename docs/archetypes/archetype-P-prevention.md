# Archetype P — Prevention & Longitudinal Guidance

**Who:** Fictional composite — "Daniel," 44, generally healthy, desk job,
family history of heart disease. Not sick, but aware he's coasting: weight
drifting up, sleep mediocre, no doctor who actually knows him. Wants a
physician relationship *before* something goes wrong, and a realistic plan
for staying well.

**Engagement shape:** ongoing, light-touch. Annual deep evaluation, periodic
check-ins, open messaging line.

---

## The proposal Daniel would see

> ### Care Proposal — prepared for Daniel · [date]
>
> **What I heard.** You're healthy today and want to stay that way. Your
> father's heart attack at 58 is on your mind. You want someone who knows
> your baseline, watches your trends, and tells you the truth about what
> matters and what doesn't — without turning your life into a medical
> project.
>
> **What I believe I can help with.** A clear picture of your actual
> cardiovascular and metabolic risk; a prevention plan you'll genuinely
> follow; catching drift early; being the doctor you call first, who already
> knows you.
>
> **The plan.**
> - **Comprehensive annual evaluation** (~90 minutes, in person): full
>   history, examination, risk assessment, screening review, and an
>   unhurried conversation about goals — with labs arranged beforehand so we
>   discuss results, not wait for them.
> - **Two scheduled check-ins** during the year (~30 min, telemedicine):
>   trends, plan adjustments, whatever's on your mind.
> - **Between visits:** message me with questions; I typically respond
>   within 1–2 business days (sooner when it matters). Results always come
>   with my interpretation, not just numbers.
> - **When you're sick:** straightforward acute issues handled by
>   telemedicine visit, usually same-week.
>
> **What we'll track** (you'll see these as trends): weight, blood
> pressure, lipids, A1c/glucose, exercise and sleep patterns, and a yearly
> well-being check — plus everything you spend, so we can both see whether
> this is worth it.
>
> **Duration & checkpoints.** One year at a time. At each annual
> evaluation we look at the trends, what this cost you, and decide together
> whether to renew.
>
> **Included:** everything above — visits, messaging, results review, care
> coordination, records access.
> **Not included:** labs, imaging, medications, vaccines, specialists, ER
> and hospital care (these run through your insurance as they do now — you
> keep and use your insurance for everything outside our arrangement),
> after-hours/weekend coverage, and issues requiring more intensive
> management — if something significant develops, we'll talk honestly about
> re-scoping.
>
> **The economics.** Annual arrangement: **~$1,300/year** — about $108/month.
> This reflects roughly 7 hours of my time on your care across the year at
> the practice's standard rate, adjusted down because your situation is
> uncomplicated. If you use much more than that, I'll tell you and we'll
> re-scope at renewal; if much less, I'll say that too.

*(Payment mechanics — monthly card-on-file vs. annual, superbill options,
whether any component could be insurance-billable — follow

## Money flow — patient's perspective

**Scenario 1 · Private-pay arrangement (default design):**

| | |
|---|---|
| Care plan price | ~$1,300/yr, paid ~$108/mo (card on file) or annually |
| What insurance still pays for | labs, imaging, medications, vaccines, specialists, urgent/ER care — unchanged from today |
| Estimated total out-of-pocket to me | ~$1,300/yr |
| Surprise-bill exposure from me | none — the price is the price |

**Scenario 2 · Hybrid (researched 2026-08; counsel still required):**
preventive/acute visits billed to insurance with a smaller private fee for
the rest — possible in principle, but the research (`payment-landscape.md`
§1) shows the fee could not lawfully describe itself as buying "extended
time, messaging, coordination" for covered care; it would have to buy a
genuinely non-covered layer. **Not offered until counsel verifies.**

**Pricing design note (researched):** at ~$108/mo, Daniel's arrangement
sits above standard Texas DPC ($79–99/mo verified) and just under the new
$150/mo HSA-eligibility cap for qualifying DPC arrangements (2026,
OBBBA) — if structured to qualify (`NEEDS-COUNSEL`, tax), he could pay
pre-tax. The proposal must make visible why this is more than volume-model
DPC: the trend tracking, the annual deep-dive, the physician who knows him.

## Physician-side economics (illustrative, $210/hr scenario rate)

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$1,300/yr (complexity 0.9 × 7.0 hr × $210) |
| Hours: sync / async / admin | 3.5 / 2.5 / 1.0 = **7.0 hr** |
| Attributable costs | ~$40 card fees (~3%); 1 rented-space session (UNSET) |
| Contribution (pre-space) | ~$1,260 → **~$180/attributable-hr** |

**Two-sided test:** Patient pays about a gym membership for a physician who
actually knows him, unhurried annual evaluation, and a watched baseline —
compare to a rushed 15-minute annual physical. Physician earns near the
target rate on the lightest-touch archetype. **Passes both sides**, provided
the async estimate holds (light patients who message heavily are the risk;
renewal re-scoping is the correction).
