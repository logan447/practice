# Archetype C — Complex Diagnostic Untangler

**Who:** Fictional composite — "Ruth," 67, eighteen months of fatigue,
intermittent palpitations, weight loss nobody can explain, three specialists
who each cleared their own organ system, a two-inch stack of records, and no
one holding the whole question. Needs someone to own the diagnostic problem
to its end.

**Engagement shape:** front-loaded first year — heavy evaluation phase, then
step-down to maintenance (usually archetype S or M pricing in year two).

**⚠ Medicare flag:** at 67, Ruth is likely Medicare-enrolled. Everything
about how this arrangement may be priced and structured for her depends on
the Medicare posture (X-04) and the hybrid-fee rules (Q-15). This file
prices the *pre-Medicare version of Ruth* (or the verified-permissible
structure, once counsel lands). The Medicare version of this archetype is a
`NEEDS-COUNSEL` design problem in its own right — flagged, not guessed.

## The proposal Ruth would see

> ### Care Proposal — prepared for Ruth · [date]
>
> **What I heard.** Something has been wrong for a year and a half. Everyone
> has ruled out their piece; no one has put the pieces together, sat with
> your full story, or told you what happens next. You are tired, worried,
> and tired of being worried.
>
> **What I believe I can help with.** Owning the whole question: one
> physician who reads everything, takes the long history, builds the
> differential, sequences the remaining workup deliberately, coordinates
> the specialists, and follows the question to an answer — or to an honest
> "here is what we know, here is what we watch."
>
> **The plan.**
> - **Records deep-dive before we start** — I read the full stack first;
>   you should not have to re-tell eighteen months from memory.
> - **Extended evaluation** (~2 hours, at your home or our clinic space):
>   complete history and examination, full review of what's been done,
>   working differential, and a written evaluation plan you can hold.
> - **Active phase (~3–4 months):** visits every 2–3 weeks (telemedicine,
>   with home visits when examination matters), each test explained before
>   and interpreted after; specialist referrals framed with precise
>   questions, their answers integrated back into one picture.
> - **Consolidation (months 4–12):** monthly, then stretching out —
>   managing what we found, watching what we're watching.
> - **Between visits:** messaging with ~1–2 business-day response; new
>   symptoms triaged promptly.
>
> **What we'll track:** the symptom picture itself (severity, pattern),
> weight, relevant labs as the workup dictates, function and energy,
> medication changes, every diagnostic step and what it cost — so the
> record shows what was done, why, what it found, and what you spent.
>
> **Duration & checkpoints.** 12 months, checkpoints at 3 and 6 months:
> is the workup converging, is the pace right, does the scope still fit.
> Year two, if you want it, is almost always a lighter, cheaper
> maintenance arrangement.
>
> **Included:** the records review, all visits including home visits
> (travel included), messaging, coordination and integration of
> specialists, written summaries after every major step.
> **Not included:** the tests, imaging, procedures, medications, and
> specialist fees themselves — those run through your insurance;
> after-hours coverage; hospital care (I stay informed and coordinate
> re-entry, but hospitalists manage admissions).
>
> **The economics.** **~$6,400 for the 12-month engagement** — about
> $535/month, or staged: a larger initial payment covering the records
> review and evaluation phase, then monthly. This is the practice's most
> intensive arrangement (~23–24 hours of my time, most of it in the first
> half), priced at the standard rate with a complexity adjustment. It is
> expensive because it is a lot of physician work — and the moment the
> question resolves into routine management, the price steps down to match.

## Money flow — patient's perspective

**Scenario 1 · Private-pay:** staged payments (e.g., initial payment at
start, then monthly) — mechanics per `payment-landscape.md`. Insurance
continues to pay for the actual diagnostics/specialists, which for this
archetype is where the *large* dollars flow; the arrangement price buys the
thinking, sequencing, and integration insurance doesn't reimburse well
anywhere.

**Scenario 2 · Hybrid (researched 2026-08):** high visit frequency and
prolonged E/M services are plausibly billable, and could meaningfully
offset the private price. For the Medicare version of Ruth the research
hardened the constraints (`payment-landscape.md` §1): claims *must* be
submitted for covered services (no cash arrangements without full opt-out,
which would end Medicare billing for every patient), and a private fee may
not cover "coordination of care" or "comprehensive assessment and plan" —
which is nearly this archetype's job description. The compliant structures
that remain (participating + genuinely-non-covered fee; opt-out + private
contracts; or no Medicare patients) are exactly the X-04 decision, with
counsel. **Market note:** ~$6,400 sits above the concierge fee band but
inside functional-medicine comprehensive-program pricing ($5–15k).

## Physician-side economics (illustrative, $210/hr scenario rate)

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$6,400 yr-1 (1.3 × 23.5 hr × $210) |
| Hours: sync / async / travel / admin | 10 / 8 / 3 / 2.5 = **23.5 hr** |
| Attributable costs | ~$190 card fees; 2 space sessions (UNSET); travel hard costs (UNSET) |
| Contribution (pre-space/travel) | ~$6,220 → **~$265/attributable-hr** — but note 3 travel hrs earn at rate only if clustering holds (R-23) |
| Year 2 step-down | typically S or M economics |

**Two-sided test:** Ruth pays real money — and receives the thing she has
not been able to buy anywhere: one competent owner of the whole question.
The fairness hinges on honest scoping (if the answer comes in month 2, the
checkpoint re-scopes rather than riding out the year). Physician earns the
highest rate here, appropriately — it is the hardest work. **Passes, with
two watch items: estimate risk (widest of any archetype) and the Medicare
design gap.**
