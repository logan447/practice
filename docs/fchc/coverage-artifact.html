Why eight months. The forgiveness rule counts a borrower full time who averages thirty hours a week across a contractual period of at least eight months, even where the rest of the year is not worked. Thirty hours across thirty-five weeks is 1,050. Sixty-three clinic days at twelve hours is 756 of them, so the remaining 294 are administrative: charting, inbox, results and coordination, about eight and a half hours a week. That is the load-bearing part of this plan. If the clinic will pay and certify those hours, the year works. If it will only certify half of them, the gap comes back as longer days or a sitter.

Why no childcare. I book a clinic day only when Jamie's schedule leaves her home with the children, so childcare never enters the plan. Count the green squares week by week and the cost of that shows itself: some weeks carry three or four, most carry two, and several carry none. The eight-month average is what absorbs the uneven weeks. A week-by-week test would not, which is why the contract period matters more than the day count.

My weeks off: Jan 1–13, Apr 1–7 spring break, May 6–19 Belize, Jul 22–Aug 11 summer, Nov 18–Dec 1 Thanksgiving, Dec 16–31 holidays. Three of those blocks fall outside the contract window altogether. Jamie's pattern: a 24-hour shift takes that day and the next; a 12-hour day takes one; five backup weeks take the whole week. She aims about 70% of her leave at my blocks.
